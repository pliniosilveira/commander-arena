# Proposal: planeswalkers (loyalty abilities, planeswalker tokens, planeswalker commanders)

Status: IMPLEMENTED on branch `m64-planeswalkers` (D-200 engine, D-201 emblems, Empower Jace and the life total, D-202 parser, D-203 agents and web; `ENGINE_VERSION` 48, `PARSER_VERSION` 52). The owner approved the whole subsystem on 2026-11-04 ("do it all at once ... a PR with full support for planeswalkers") and all ten recommendations of section 7, with the one change that emblems were built in the same work (they were a separate proposal here, section 3 D7); the eight stacked steps of section 4 were done as one branch, and the section is kept as written, so what the proposal expected can be read against what was built (the "Outcome" section at the end). Gap class: S (a new ability family with its own timing rule, a new token kind, new decisions and UI, and a long list of existing code to re-check). Date: 2026-11-04, repository at `main` commit 2a60af1, `ENGINE_VERSION` 47, `PARSER_VERSION` 51. Documentation only: no engine or parser code, no version bump, no golden change.

Source: the owner's decision of 2026-11-04 ("we need to start supporting planeswalkers"), before the next deck, the Vraska deck of GitHub issue 111: its commander, Vraska, Soul of Stone, may be a planeswalker (nothing in the repository says: unverified), and two of its cards, Protege's Awakening ("Empower Jace 6.") and Keeper of the Quiet Hour ("When ~ enters, empower Jace 2."), create a Jace planeswalker token (CR 701.71a). The deck analysis that frames those cards is `docs/proposals/vraska-deck-analysis.md` on the branch `vraska-deck-analysis` (not merged when this was written; section 4.2 there recommended leaving the two cards out and deciding the subsystem later; the owner has now decided).

## 0. The short version

- **The engine is further along than the earlier analyses said.** Planeswalkers exist as a card type since M2 (D-043): they enter with loyalty counters, are attacked (the attacker chooses a player or a planeswalker for each attacker), are blocked for, lose loyalty from damage, go to the graveyard at 0 loyalty, and the web already draws their loyalty and lets an attacker be sent at one. All of that is tested with SYNTHETIC vanilla planeswalkers. What does not exist is everything a real planeswalker has on top: loyalty abilities (CR 606: no cost, no timing rule, no AST support, no parser line), planeswalker tokens (a token made with loyalty), the target "player or planeswalker", planeswalker subtypes in the type table, the AI using or defending one, and a commander that is a planeswalker ("can be your commander" is read by deck validation, not by the parser).
- **Recommendation.** Build the subsystem (option A of the Vraska analysis) in eight stacked steps, additive, no big refactor: a loyalty ability is an ordinary `activated` ability with a new cost kind `loyalty`; the engine derives "loyalty ability" from that cost (CR 606.2), forces sorcery timing, and remembers "a loyalty ability of this permanent was activated this turn" in the existing per-turn log. The attack-target model stays as it is. Total estimate for steps 1 to 8: about 0.85 of the Dragons slice (engine and parser together = 1.0), without emblems (about 0.3, its own proposal) and without flip walkers and Compleated (about 0.15).
- **The main risks** are not in what is added but in what real cards will reach for the first time: the damage-order pipeline (D-191, D-192) with a planeswalker as recipient, the combat code with a planeswalker that changes controller or stops being a planeswalker (found while reading, listed in section 5), and the effect grammar of real loyalty lines (an offline sample reads about half, section 3).
- **What the owner decides** is in section 7. The first one is cheap and decisive: run the extraction of `docs/proposals/planeswalker-acceptance-names.txt` (and `pnpm corpus:decks` on the Vraska deck) so that the real text of Vraska, Soul of Stone and of Jace-type loyalty lines is in the repository before anything is built.

## 1. Rules (the repository's CR, read with `pnpm rule`)

The CR in `docs/rules/` is the one effective September 25, 2026 (`docs/rules/RULES_VERSION.md`), plus the Update Bulletin of October 19, 2026, which added seven planeswalker subtypes to 205.3j (Arzakon, Dyfed, Feroz, Greensleeves, Sifa, Thomil, Worzel). Two old rules are gone in this text and must NOT be implemented:

- **CR 306.4** says the "planeswalker uniqueness rule" (one planeswalker of each type per player) was removed; older cards got the legendary supertype in Oracle, so the legend rule (CR 704.5j) covers them. A player may control two Jace planeswalkers that are not legendary (the Jace token of CR 701.71a is not said to be).
- **CR 306.7** says the old redirection of noncombat damage from a player to a planeswalker was removed; cards were errata'd to deal damage to a planeswalker directly. There is no "redirect to a planeswalker" rule to build. (CR 614.9 still lets a redirection effect, such as Ancient Adamantoise, name a planeswalker as a recipient, which the engine already allows.)

The rules that do apply:

| Rule | What it says (short) |

### What a real loyalty line looks like to the parser today

Probed on 2026-11-04 (scratch script, not committed): the lines "+1: Create a 1/1 white Soldier creature token.", "−2: ~ deals 3 damage to any target.", "0: Draw a card.", "−7: You get an emblem with ...", "[+1]: Draw a card." are each `unrecognised`, closest rule none, on a planeswalker face and on a creature face. The same effect text behind a `{T}:` cost reads.

To size how much of the effect side reads, the 26 loyalty-shaped templates of the owner's `reports/templates.json` (the 1,000 heaviest unread templates at parser version 34, 36 abilities) were replayed with the loyalty prefix replaced by `{T}:`: 14 of 26 templates (24 of 36 abilities) read. The ones that do not need work that is not planeswalker work: emblems (three of the 12 failures: "You get an emblem with ...", no emblem objects exist), "face down and look at it", "Search ... with mana value N or less", "becomes a Treasure artifact with ...", "Until your next turn, you may cast sorcery spells as though they had flash". This is an estimate on a biased sample (the heaviest templates only, an old parser version, and the long tail of planeswalkers is not in it): it says the loyalty-line prefix is the first blocker and the effect grammar is the second, not how many planeswalkers will be supported. The count of real planeswalkers in Commander cannot be taken here (no bulk file); the owner's `pnpm coverage:cards` run would give it.

## 3. Design options

### D1. How a loyalty ability is represented

- (a) A new ability kind `loyalty { change, targets, effect, ... }` next to `activated` and `mana`. Rejected: every place that reads `kind === 'activated'` (derive, `activatedAbilitiesOf`, the view's `activated` list, ability objects on the stack, the web's labels, the AI's allowlist, the validator) would miss it silently or need a second copy of its branch; targets, modes, `costReducedIf` and `activateOnlyIf` would be repeated.
- (b) **Recommended.** An `activated` ability whose `cost` holds a new cost kind `loyalty { change: number | 'x' }` (positive puts counters, negative removes, 0 does neither, `'x'` is "-X"). CR 606.2 defines a loyalty ability exactly this way ("an activated ability with a loyalty symbol in its cost"), so the engine derives the property from the cost and nothing else needs a flag. The validator forces `timing: 'sorcery'` for it, allows at most one loyalty cost per ability, refuses it on a face that is not a planeswalker (granted loyalty abilities are rare and left out), and refuses it together with `oncePerTurn` (606.3 is the rule). Activation legality adds 606.3 (below) and 606.6 (the existing "fewer than N counters" check, with the cost as adjusted). Payment adds or removes `loyalty` counters through the code the counter costs already use.
- (c) Reuse `putCounters` and `removeCounters` with `counter: 'loyalty'` plus a boolean on the ability. Rejected: the validator wants `count >= 1` (a "0" ability does not fit), X does not fit, and CR 606.4 and 606.5 modifiers ("cost an additional [+1]") apply to loyalty symbols, not to counter costs in general.
- The once-per-permanent rule: `state.turnLog.activated` entries get a `loyalty: true` mark when the ability activated has a loyalty cost, written at activation, and `activationRefusal` refuses any loyalty ability of an object that has such an entry this turn. A permanent that left and came back is a new object (CR 400.7). The `oncePerTurn` mechanism is per ability and is not reused.

### D2. How an attack's defender is represented

No choice to make: `AttackTarget` (player or permanent) and `combat.attackers[].target` already carry it, and `defenderOf` reads "defending player" from it (CR 508.5). Recommendation: keep it. Changing it would touch every attack reader for no gain. The work is in the edges (section 5): put-onto-the-battlefield-attacking (508.4), trample over planeswalkers, and removal from combat when the planeswalker changes controller or type.

### D3. The target "player or planeswalker"

- (a) **Recommended.** A new `TargetSpec.what` kind `{ kind: 'playerOrObject', player: PlayerFilter, filter: ObjectFilter }` (the second filter must name only planeswalkers, battles or both); `legalTargets` returns the players and the matching permanents; a `ChosenTarget` is already either a player or an object, so nothing downstream changes. The parser's `noun.player-or-planeswalker` lowers to it.
- (b) `anyTarget` plus a narrowing filter ("any target that is a player or a planeswalker"). Rejected: `anyTarget` has no filter, and adding one makes the node mean two things.
- (c) Keep reading it as a player. This is today's behaviour (D-180) and is a rules deviation as soon as a planeswalker is real: the player cannot aim Lavalanche at a planeswalker. Not acceptable after step 4.

### D4. How the web shows loyalty abilities and the attack at a planeswalker

- (a) **Recommended first.** No new dialog kind. The existing chooser (click the permanent) lists its loyalty abilities as "+1: ...", "-2: ...", "0: ..." with the cost worded as the CR writes it (a new case in `game/cost-text.ts` and `ui/decisions/ability-label.ts`), the abilities the engine does not list are simply absent, and the tile gets a small "used" mark while the permanent has activated one this turn (the view needs that one boolean). Loyalty stays in the corner. Printed loyalty is added to `ObjectView` so a card in hand shows it.
- (b) A planeswalker card face with the abilities as clickable rows. Better to look at, but a new component and a new layout; deferred until the owner has played with (a).
- The attack at a planeswalker needs no new UI: the declaration already lets each attacker choose; a browser test is added (none exists for planeswalkers).

### D5. How the AI values and uses planeswalkers

The AI decks are the precons and the arena decks (no planeswalker in any), and the human plays the planeswalkers, so the AI mostly has to attack the human's planeswalkers and answer them, and rarely to use its own. Recommendation:

- Casual agent: keep the player as the default target; add one rule: when an opposing planeswalker could be killed this combat by attackers that are safe, send them at it (the heuristic agent already builds that candidate). For its own planeswalker (only when the owner brings an AI a planeswalker deck): activate at most one loyalty ability a turn, chosen by a small table (a plus ability first unless the minus kills something worth more, never an ability that leaves 0 loyalty unless it is an ultimate it can afford), using the existing `modeScore` for targets. Documented as limited.
- Heuristic agent: for each legal loyalty `activate` option, play it out on the sample and read the evaluator (the pattern of the vote in D-194); defend: blocks read attacks at its planeswalkers as well as at itself (`heuristic-blockplan.ts` reads only `atMe`).
- Evaluator: keep the flat value plus loyalty; revisit after arena games with a SYNTHETIC planeswalker deck style (soak, D-126). The owner's AI-strength work stays on hold.
- Rejected: an AI that never uses its planeswalkers at all. It is the cheapest and it is honest, but the random agent (which picks legal actions) would use them and the casual agent would look broken the first time the owner gives it one.

### D6. How a planeswalker token is made

- (a) **Recommended.** The generic fix first: `createTokens` gives a token its loyalty counters at creation (CR 306.5b applies to every planeswalker, not only to cards), and a token copy of a planeswalker likewise. Then `empowerJace { amount }` as an effect (the keyword action of CR 701.71a, in the style of `ringTempts`) builds the token from an engine constant written from the CR text (like `TOKEN_PRESETS`), asks the controller which Jace token to put the counters on when they control more than one (`choosePermanent`, a purpose of its own), and puts N loyalty counters on it. No state-based check runs between the creation and the counters because both happen in one resolution (CR 704.3), which a test pins.
- (b) Only the Jace token as a special case (option B of the Vraska analysis). Rejected there and here: the token would be a planeswalker that cannot be attacked or targeted unless most of the subsystem exists anyway.

### D7. Emblems

The ultimates of many planeswalkers ("-7: You get an emblem with ...") need emblems. The engine has none (the Ring is a record on the seat, D-196, with fixed abilities). The general design is a new object kind in the command zone (CR 114.1) whose abilities `derive` reads, which is its own S-class proposal. Recommendation: not in this program. A planeswalker whose ability makes an emblem stays unsupported (the line stays `unrecognised`, honest).

## 4. Plan

Each step is one branch and one pull request, stacked in order, in the style of the earlier programs: scenario tests first, then the engine, then the parser, the web wording and the docs; `ENGINE_VERSION` and `PARSER_VERSION` bump where a game or a parse changes; the precon fingerprint (`node tools/perf/precon-digest.ts`) must be identical before and after wherever no planeswalker is in play (every precon), and `node tools/perf/derive-bench.ts` must not regress. Branch names take the next free `m<n>`. Sizes are my estimates, in the unit of the earlier analyses: the Dragons slice (D-151 and D-152, engine and parser together) = 1.0. No step is a behaviour-preserving refactor: unlike the control change (`docs/design/control-change.md`), nothing existing has to be rewritten first, so the "refactor first" slot is empty; the precon fingerprint is the guard that stays.

## 5. Risks

- **Combat (steps 4 and 5).** The attack model is sound and tested, but reading it for this proposal found three untested edges. (1) `defenderOf` reads the planeswalker's current controller (`controllerOf`): if a control-changing effect (D-189) takes an attacked planeswalker, its new controller is the attacker's, so the "defending player" becomes the attacker and the attackers may be blocked by their own side; CR 506.4 removes a planeswalker that changes controller from combat (the attackers stay in combat, 506.4c). Not verified by a test: reachable today with a SYNTHETIC planeswalker and a `gainControl` effect. (2) `combat-removal.ts` handles creatures only; a planeswalker that is attacked and stops being a planeswalker (506.4e) is not removed. (3) A creature that is also a planeswalker (Gideon-like) both attacks and is attacked: 506.4d. All three need scenario tests before any real card with the shape exists.
- **The damage-order pipeline (D-191, D-192).** The question of which effect to apply first goes to the controller of the damaged permanent, which for a planeswalker is its controller; the pipeline's memoised search over orders (`damage-order.ts`) was written and tested for creatures and players. A planeswalker adds recipients that are neither (counters removed, no marked damage), redirects that name it and shields "to target planeswalker". Each combination is a scenario in step 4. A change to `dealDamage` is a change to every supported card with damage: the precon fingerprint is the check.
- **The activation code.** `rules/activate*.ts` is used by every supported activated ability; the new cost and the per-permanent mark must not change a game without a planeswalker (the fingerprint), and the cancel and rewind logic of an activation (a cancel before the last cost "rewinds both", D-185) must rewind a loyalty payment as well: a test.
- **The AI.** Two things can go wrong: the casual agent ignores a planeswalker it controls (honest, documented) or the random agent activates abilities with targets it cannot judge (fine, it is random); the heuristic agent's simulation multiplies its branches by the number of loyalty options (the time bound of D-164 and D-172 applies; a planeswalker with four abilities adds four branches to a main phase). The soak deck style must run inside the budget.
- **The grammar.** About half of a sample of loyalty effect lines reads today. A planeswalker "supported" by step 3 means "every line read and passed `validateCard`", which for most real planeswalkers is false until the effect grammar grows; the owner should expect the first run after step 3 to move few real cards, and the backlog to name the effect shapes next. This is the honest outcome, not a failure of the step.
- **Rules from memory.** No rulings could be read (Scryfall is blocked). Points the CR text leaves to rulings and that the owner can check: a loyalty ability's cost when the permanent has fewer counters than the "-X" the player announces, a loyalty ability copied or granted (CR 606.2 "normally"), an ability that reads the loyalty of a planeswalker that has left (last-known information, CR 113.7a), "Planeswalker" cards turned into creatures by Oath-like effects.
- **Documentation drift.** Two earlier statements are out of date and step 7 corrects them: the Vraska analysis (section 4.2: "the engine's attack declaration chooses a player only") and D-180's "the engine has no planeswalker permanents" (true for real cards only).

## 7. Open questions for the owner, with my recommendation

1. **Build the whole subsystem now, or only what the Vraska deck needs?** Recommendation: the whole subsystem, in the order of section 4. ## Outcome (written when the work was done, 2026-11-04)

What differed from the plan, and what the real texts showed:

- **One branch, one pull request**, as the owner asked, instead of eight stacked ones; four decisions instead of one for each step (D-200 to D-203).
- **Emblems were built** (D7 said later): the command zone and `derive` made it small once the object kind existed (D-201): a `card: null` object with a `token` record and `emblem: true`, its static abilities read with the permanents' and its triggers scanned with the graveyard's. The Ring stays a record on the seat.
- **The model.** Tokens, token copies, copies of permanent spells and permanents that enter as copies get their loyalty (step 1); the planeswalker types are generated from CR 205.3j and the applied bulletin (87 names), not typed; `ObjectView` has the printed loyalty and a used mark. Planeswalker faces of double-faced cards: the engine runs a planeswalker that transforms in place but refuses a planeswalker back face (`validate-layouts.ts`), and none of the three flip walkers of the corpus reads for other reasons (a transform trigger on loyalty counters, "exile ~, then return him transformed", a damage-equal-to-power line): left unsupported and listed in the ROADMAP.
- **Loyalty abilities** (step 2) as designed (D1 b): `activated` with the cost `loyalty`. The validator does not force `timing: 'sorcery'`; the engine derives the timing from the cost, which keeps the parser's output free of a flag it would have to remember. `[-X]` needed an X that is a loyalty amount: `XCost.loyalty`.
- **The parser** (step 3) read the real lines at once because the sign is U+2212 and there are no brackets in Oracle text (the brackets of the rules text are read too). The effect side was the long pole, as the proposal said: five real planeswalkers and the two Jace cards read completely (D-202 lists them and what each stops at).
- **Targets and damage** (step 4): the target `playerOrObject` (D3 a) and Lavalanche's real reading; damage to a planeswalker with every damage modifier already worked, and is now pinned by scenarios (shields, the order question of D-191, redirection of D-192, infect, lifelink, a creature that is a planeswalker).
- **Combat** (step 5): the three untested edges were real: the defending player now follows the planeswalker's controller at declaration, a stolen or retyped planeswalker is removed from combat, and the animated planeswalker attacks as a creature and is attacked as both. Myriad and enter-attacking stay at players (question 7), trample over planeswalkers is not a keyword (question 8).
- **Web and AI** (step 6) as in D4 a and D5: the chooser, the used mark, printed loyalty, the emblem in the command zone, the minimal agents, a soak style, a browser test.
- **Recheck** (step 7): Lavalanche, the D-192 redirect recipients (tested with a planeswalker), the ROADMAP and RULES_COVERAGE rows, the Vraska analysis (planeswalkers are done) and the "no real planeswalker is supported" notes.
- **Empower Jace and the commander** (step 8): done; Theorist's Sanctum (a behold in an enters replacement) and Forge of Heroes ("choose target commander that entered this turn", a loyalty counter if it is a planeswalker) did not become readable, for reasons that are not about planeswalkers.
- **Real cards.** The golden corpus (138 acceptance cards, the owner's extraction of 2026-11-04) went from 73 to 80 supported; training and hold-out did not move (162 of 192 and 52 of 61). Garruk Wildspeaker, Jace Beleren, Elspeth, Sun's Champion, Gideon, Ally of Zendikar, Vraska, Relic Seeker, Protege's Awakening and Keeper of the Quiet Hour read and run; the other planeswalkers stop at one line each (D-202).
