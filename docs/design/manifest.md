# Design: manifest and face-down permanents (S-class, approved)

Status: APPROVED by the owner in chat on 2026-11-12 ("Manifest is needed. I'll use other cards with this ability."), recorded in `docs/proposals/niv-mizzet-owner-decisions.md`. Provisional decision numbers D-961 to D-969 (the integrator renumbers them). The rule text below was read with `pnpm rule` from the CR copy of the owner's private rules repository; quotes are from that text and the rest is paraphrase with the rule number. The card text (Reality Shift) is the real Scryfall text of the golden corpus (issue 25).

## What this builds, and how it relates to `docs/proposals/face-down.md`

`face-down.md` (approved at low priority, Morph, Megamorph, Disguise) designs the machinery of face-down permanents in six steps and lists manifest, cloak and manifest dread as "not in this proposal". The owner now needs manifest, so this build covers those steps as far as manifest needs them, and adds manifest itself. It does not contradict `face-down.md`: where that text names a name or shape (`GameObject.faceDown?: { by: ... }`, an action `turnFaceUp { object }`, the label "Face-down creature N", a `revealed` event) this build uses it.

| Step of `face-down.md` | This build |
| --- | --- |
| 1. State and layer 1b | Built: `GameObject.faceDown`, layer 1b in `copiableValues` (hence in `derive`, `typeViewOf` and every reader of characteristics). `by` is `'manifest'` only; the type is a union the later kinds extend. |
| 2. Casting face down (Morph, Megamorph, Disguise: {3} alternative cost, face-down spell on the stack) | NOT built. The state can also sit on a stack object (a face-down spell enters the battlefield face down, CR 708.4) and `moveObject` carries `faceDown` over a stack-to-battlefield move, so the door is open, but nothing makes one. |
| 3. Turning face up | Built for manifest: the action `turnFaceUp`, a special action paid with a `payMana` decision of purpose `turnFaceUp` (as Suspend and Foretell are). The cost comes from the kind (`faceDownTurnUpCost`): for manifest the card's own mana cost (CR 701.40b); Morph's morph cost, Megamorph's counter (CR 708.11) and Disguise's ward would be other branches of that one function. The trigger kind "When ~ is turned face up" is NOT built: a manifested card's turned-face-up abilities are not read by any card of the owner's decks and manifest needs none; it is listed in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. The `turnedFaceUp` event is emitted, so adding the trigger later reads it. |
| 4. Hidden information | Built in full (the core risk): views, events, the log, stack and decision texts, `determinize`, replays, and the web. See "Hidden information". |
| 5. AI | Built for turning face up (Casual and Heuristic); casting face down is not built. |
| 6. Version and soak | A SYNTHETIC deck style and a 20-game soak (D-126) that checks no event or view names a face-down card for the opponent. `ENGINE_VERSION` and `PARSER_VERSION` are bumped by the integrator, not here. |

Not in scope, said plainly: Morph, Megamorph, Disguise, "cloak" (CR 701.58: the same as manifest with ward {2} on the permanent; `by: 'cloak'` and a ward expansion would do it) and "manifest dread" (CR 701.62: look at the top two cards, manifest one, put the other into the graveyard; it needs a choice of a card, which is a new use of an existing `chooseCards` decision). Nothing here designs them out: `faceDown.by` is the one switch they would extend, and the characteristics, the hiding and the turning face up are written in terms of `by`.

## Rules (read with `pnpm rule`)

- CR 701.40a (Manifest): "To manifest a card, turn it face down. It becomes a 2/2 face-down creature card with no text, no name, no subtypes, and no mana cost. Put that card onto the battlefield face down. That permanent is a manifested permanent for as long as it remains face down." The effect defining its characteristics ends when it is turned face up.
- CR 701.40b: any time you have priority, you may turn a manifested permanent you control face up: a special action, no stack (CR 116.2b); show all players that the card is a creature card and what its mana cost is, pay that cost, turn it face up. A card that is not a creature card, or has no mana cost, can't be turned face up this way.
- CR 701.40e: several cards are manifested one at a time. CR 701.40f: if a rule or effect stops the face-down object entering, the card is not manifested and stays where it was, face up (the engine has no such prohibition yet; the effect leaves the card in the library if the move does not complete). CR 701.40g: a manifested instant or sorcery that "would turn face up" is revealed and stays face down: the engine has no effect that turns a permanent face up except the special action, which is refused for it (701.40b), so nothing to build; noted.
- CR 708.2, 708.2a, 613.2b (layer 1b): a face-down permanent has only the characteristics the rules give it, and they are its copiable values, applied as layer 1b (after copy effects, before everything else). A permanent that enters face down has them too. CR 708.2b: a face-down permanent can't be turned face down (no effect of the engine tries).
- CR 708.3: it is turned face down before it enters, so its own enters abilities and replacement effects do not apply (the card's face is not looked at as it enters: no "enters tapped", no counters, no choices, no triggers of its own).
- CR 708.5: its controller may look at it at any time; no other player may (a permanent that phased out too). CR 708.6: the controller must be able to tell their face-down permanents apart: each has a label ("Face-down creature N") that stays with the object.
- CR 708.8: turned face up, its copiable values revert to the card's own; effects already applied (counters, pumps, a copy effect) still apply; enters abilities do not trigger.
- CR 708.9: a face-down permanent that moves from the battlefield to another zone is revealed as it moves; at the end of the game all face-down permanents are revealed (the web shows the real cards of both sides in the end screen log; the engine's `gameEnded` result is unchanged).
- CR 708.10: a face-down permanent that becomes a copy keeps the face-down characteristics (layer 1b is applied after the copy), and if turned face up it has what it copied; a copy of a face-down permanent copies the face-down characteristics (a face-up 2/2 with no name, CR 707.2).
- CR 903.9, 903.3 (commander): a commander card that is manifested is still a commander card (it is the same card); leaving the battlefield is the usual zone change with the usual choice.
- CR 400.7: leaving the battlefield (or any zone change) makes a new object; the new object is face up: `faceDown` is on the object, not on the card.

## State

`GameObject.faceDown?: { readonly by: FaceDownBy; readonly n: number }` with `FaceDownBy = 'manifest'`. It is on the battlefield object (and phased-out objects, which keep every field). `n` is the label number: the smallest positive integer no other face-down permanent on the battlefield or phased out has, so the labels in play are unique and a permanent keeps its label for as long as it is face down, which is what CR 708.6 asks. It is a designation, not a copiable value, and a new object after any zone change has none. It is read only through `rules/face-down.ts` (`isFaceDown`, `faceDownLabel`, `faceDownTurnUpCost`), as `paired` is read only through `pairing.ts`.

## Layer 1b

`copiableValues(state, obj)` (the first thing `derive`, `typeViewOf`, `oracleTextOf`, `recordLki` and the copy effects read) applies, after the copy effects: for a face-down object, the values of `FACE_DOWN_VALUES` in `rules/face-down.ts`: no name, no mana cost, no supertypes, types `['creature']`, no subtypes, no colors, power 2, toughness 2, no abilities, no text. Every later layer works on them as on any values (counters, pumps, "becomes", control, abilities granted by other permanents), which is what CR 613.1 and 708.8 want. A copy effect that copies a face-down permanent reads these values (CR 707.2); a face-down permanent that becomes a copy keeps them while face down (CR 708.10), because the override is applied last.

Readers that look at the card's printed face directly instead of the values (a few places that read `definitionOf(...).faces[...]`) were checked one by one for the battlefield: the ones that can see a face-down permanent are `view.ts` (masked, below), `rules/zones.ts` (entering: skipped for a face-down entry), `rules/transform.ts` (a face-down permanent can't be transformed: refused), `rules/layouts.ts` and `derive-view.ts` `manaCostOf` (the back-face and meld cost: skipped), and the commander readers (below). What is left is pinned by the hidden-information audit below, which fails when any of them names a hidden card.

## The manifest effect

`{ kind: 'manifest'; player: PlayerSelector; fromLibraryOf?: PlayerSelector }`. `player` is who manifests (CR 701.40a: the player who puts it onto the battlefield, so the one who controls it, CR 110.2a); `fromLibraryOf` is whose library, the same player when absent ("the top card of their library", "your library"). "Its controller manifests the top card of their library" is `{ player: controllerOf(target), }`: the manifesting player is the controller (last-known information of the exiled permanent, as for every other "its controller" effect: `PlayerSelector.controllerOf` already reads `state.lki`). An empty library manifests nothing (no loss, CR 121.4 is about drawing).

Resolution (`rules/manifest.ts`, `manifestTop`): the top card of the library is moved with `moveObject(state, { object, to: 'battlefield', toSeat: manifesting seat, faceDown: { by: 'manifest' } })`. `moveObject` creates the object face down. Because it is turned face down before it enters (CR 708.3) the entering code does not read the card's face: no "enters tapped", counters, planeswalker loyalty, battle defense, choices, riot, devour, prepared or copy-as-enters; "enters with" group effects of other permanents (an Angel lord's counters) and the turn log see the face-down creature. The events are `zoneChanged` with `card: null` (the card is hidden: `GameEvent.zoneChanged.card` is `null` for an entry face down, so no event names the card), and then `manifested { seat, object }`. The commander replacement of CR 903.9b is about hand and library and does not apply to library to battlefield. The effect needs no decision, so no new decision kind. It reports `performed` 1 when a card was manifested.

Why `fromLibraryOf` and not only the actor: "manifest the top card of target opponent's library" exists in the game (the opponent's card goes face down under your control); the parser reads the actor forms the owner needs (`its controller manifests the top card of their library`, `manifest the top card of your library`, `target player manifests the top card of their library`) and the engine already supports the other. The parser does not read "of target opponent's library" until a card brings it (limits).

## Turning face up

`Action { kind: 'turnFaceUp'; object }`, `PriorityOption { kind: 'turnFaceUp'; object }`, offered in a priority decision for every face-down permanent its controller may turn up and can pay for (`rules/face-down.ts`, `canTurnFaceUp`: on the battlefield, controlled by the seat (`controllerOf`), not phased out, `by` allows it, the card is a creature card whose front face has a mana cost, the seat can pay it now; an {X} in the cost is 0, CR 107.3). It needs no timing but priority (CR 116.2b), so it is offered whenever the seat has priority, also with the stack not empty and in other players' turns. `applyAction` routes it as `beginSpecialAction` routes Suspend and Foretell: a `payMana` decision of purpose `turnFaceUp` with `source` the permanent and `totalCost` the cost (a cost of {0} is taken at once); `payAuto`/`payManual` pays and turns it up; `cancel` gives priority back unchanged. It is not a new decision kind.

Taking it (CR 701.40b, 708.8): the object is patched (`faceDown` removed: same object, same id, same counters, attachments, damage, timestamp, summoning-sickness status and effects), then `turnedFaceUp { seat, object, card }` is emitted (public now: the permanent is face up). No `zoneChanged` event, no enters trigger (CR 708.8). Priority returns to the same seat with the passes cleared (CR 116.1), like the other special actions. Layer 1b ends, the card's own characteristics and abilities apply from then on, and a static ability of the card that works on the battlefield starts working (a lord, say); triggers that were "waiting" do not exist (nothing triggered from the face-down object).

The cost is shown to the other players by the `turnedFaceUp` event and the log at the end of the action (the card is public then); a player who starts the action and cancels reveals nothing in the engine, which is the one place this differs from "show all players ... what that card's mana cost is" (CR 701.40b): the engine asks for the payment of a decision shown to the controller alone, and a cancelled payment changes nothing. Recorded as a limit.

## Leaving the battlefield, copies, commanders, other rules

- Leaving: `moveObject` makes a new, face-up object in the new zone from the same card (CR 400.7), so the card is revealed by moving. The events carry the card (`zoneChanged.card`, `died.card`) and the log names it: CR 708.9 says it is revealed as it moves. Last-known information of the face-down permanent has the face-down characteristics (derive), so "whenever a creature you control dies" sees a 2/2 creature; counters and attachments are lost or fall off as for any permanent. `recordLki` keeps `faceDown` in the object it stores.
- Exile with Reality Shift: the object is exiled and the controller (read from last-known information) manifests; a face-down target is revealed in the exile like any other card leaving.
- Phasing: the object keeps `faceDown`; a phased-out face-down permanent is hidden in the same way (the view masks by `faceDown`, not by zone) and `determinize` treats it like a battlefield one.
- Control change: the controller (derived, `controllerOf`) is the one who may look and turn it up; the hiding is relative to the controller at the time of the view, and the label stays.
- Copying: pinned with tests (a copy of a face-down permanent is a face-up 2/2 with no name; a face-down permanent that becomes a copy keeps the 2/2 and, turned face up, is the copy).
- Commander: a commander card manifested from a library is the same card; `commanderCardOf` still finds it (commander damage is tracked by card, CR 903.10a), and the view hides `isCommander` for the other players. Limit (in `docs/ROADMAP.md`): the table of commander damage, which is public and keyed by card, would name a face-down commander once it deals combat damage. A commander can only be in a library after its owner declined the command-zone replacement, which is rare, and manifesting it from there rarer.
- Transform: a face-down permanent can't be transformed (the engine refuses it, like CR 701.28f for a permanent that is not a transforming double-faced one); a double-faced card turned face up shows its front face.
- Tokens: a token can't be manifested (it has no card); a copy of a face-down permanent is a token or a copy effect with the face-down values, not face down itself.
- The end of the game reveals everything in the web's log (CR 708.9); the engine state keeps the cards, so `replayGame` is exact.

## Hidden information (the core risk)

The principle that makes this tractable: the engine's own characteristics for a face-down permanent ARE the face-down ones (layer 1b), so everything that reads characteristics through `derive` or `copiableValues` (names, types, mana value, abilities, text, last-known information, the sources of abilities on the stack, decisions' `sourceName`) cannot name the card. What can is code that reads the card (`state.cards`, `definitionOf`) or the card id. Those were listed and handled one by one; the rest of the defence is a test that fails when a new one appears.

| Surface | Defence |
| --- | --- |
| `getView(state, viewer)` `ObjectView` of a face-down permanent | For every viewer: `name` is "Face-down creature N", the characteristics are the derived ones (a 2/2 creature plus whatever later layers did), `manaCost` null, `oracleText` empty, no `faces`/`layout`/`meld`, `isCommander` false, `support` 'supported' (an unsupported hidden card would show itself by its badge), `castCost`/`alternativeCosts` absent, `faceDown: true`, `faceDownBy: 'manifest'` (public: the effect that made it is public, CR 708.6). For its controller only: `hiddenFace` (the card's real printed face, `FaceView`) and `turnFaceUpCost` (the mana the special action would cost, present only when the card can be turned up at all). A `moves` view of an opponent's face-down permanent equals a view of any other face-down creature with the same derived values. |
| Events (`applyAction` result, replays, the web log) | `zoneChanged.card` is `null` for an entry face down. No other event names a hidden card: events carry object ids, the object of a face-down permanent is a plain id with no name. `manifested { seat, object }` names no card. `turnedFaceUp { seat, object, card }` names the card, public by then. Leaving the battlefield reveals (CR 708.9). The test `face-down-events.test.ts` classifies every `GameEvent` kind (a `Record<GameEvent['kind'], ...>` that fails to compile for a new kind) by whether it can carry a card or a name, and a dynamic audit runs real games and fails when any event of a seat's action names a card that is face down at that moment. |
| Stack, targets, trigger order, `waitingOn` | Names come from `sourceInfo` (derive: no name; the view and decisions show the label) and from last-known information; the test checks the views of a stack with a face-down source and targets. |
| `determinize` (the AI's sample) | The face-down permanents of other controllers are hidden slots: their cards are pooled with the owner's hand and library cards and dealt again, so the sample is a legal world that agrees with the view. The permanent keeps its label, counters and everything public. The viewer's own face-down permanents are known and untouched. |
| Decisions | A `PendingDecision` goes only to its seat (existing rule). `payMana` of `turnFaceUp` names the permanent and the cost to its controller alone. Other decisions about an opponent's face-down permanent (as a target) carry object ids only. |
| The web | The Worker owns the state; the page gets views and lines. Log lines are made from events and the states around them; `nameIn` (`game/describe.ts`) gives a face-down permanent its label, never its card, and the new lines word manifest and turning up ("manifests the top card of their library", "turns a face-down card face up"). The image is the Manifest token's, not the card's. The controller sees the real card in the preview panel only, with a "face-down" marker. |
| Replays and logs | The action log has actions, not names; the `turnFaceUp` action names an object id. |
| Commander damage | See "Leaving the battlefield...": a limit. |

The audit (engine, `test/face-down-hidden.test.ts`; AI soak, `packages/ai/test/manifest-games.test.ts` and an extension of `hidden-info.test.ts`): for every state of the games and every viewer, the secrets (the card name, oracle text, card id, definition's oracle id and the object ids of the card in its hidden zones) of every face-down permanent that the viewer may not look at must not appear anywhere in the JSON of the viewer's `GameView`, and no event of the action names them (while they are face down). Both audits count what they checked, so a vacuous pass fails.

## The AI

Agents see only their view, so they cannot peek: an opponent's face-down permanent is the 2/2 with a label. The new answers are only the priority option `turnFaceUp`: the Casual agent turns a creature up when it can pay and the face-up creature is better than a 2/2 (it prefers to do it with mana that would otherwise go unused, in the main phase, or before combat when the real card has better power and toughness, evasion or a keyword that wins an attack or a block, and in response to nothing special); the Heuristic agent evaluates the board with the card's real face (its own `hiddenFace`) against the 2/2 and turns up before declaring attackers when that is worth the mana, before blockers when it wins a block, and never when the mana is needed for a spell it would rather cast (the same budget the main-phase planner already uses). A manifested non-creature card is never offered. Attacking with and blocking by a face-down creature use its derived 2/2. The random agent already picks among the legal actions. The decision switch needs no handler for the payment: `payMana` of purpose `turnFaceUp` is answered by the existing payment handlers (`payAuto`).

## The parser

The verb "manifest" in the effect rules (`effect.player`: the actor forms `its controller manifests the top card of their library`, `you manifest the top card of your library`, `target player manifests the top card of their library`; `effect.manifest`: the imperative "Manifest the top card of your library"), class T, lowered to the engine effect, each with `examples`, `rejects` and `cites` (CR 701.40a). The reminder text of the card ("(That player puts the top card ... face down as a 2/2 creature. ...)") is a parenthesis and is dropped like every reminder text. Reality Shift reads as supported (validateCard clean); the golden corpus snapshot moves only for it. Not read, and so unsupported: "manifest dread", "cloak", "manifest the top two cards", "manifest ... of target opponent's library", "manifested" as a condition and "turned face up" triggers.

## The web

A face-down permanent is drawn as a card back with the image of Scryfall's "Manifest" token (a 2/2 creature token of Fate Reforged, collector number 4), fetched by the page at run time like every card image, through a small isolated function (`apps/web/src/ui/board/face-down-art.ts`) that holds the lookup: `GET https://api.scryfall.com/cards/tfrf/4` (set code `tfrf`, Fate Reforged Tokens, collector number `4`), reading `image_uris` from the answer at run time and caching it for the session. The lookup is VERIFIED: the coordinator checked it from a GitHub runner on 2026-11-12 (the cloud session itself cannot reach Scryfall): HTTP 200, id `e9f896d4-34b4-407d-be7e-bf7a69aaf811`, oracle id `f4f184ef-f456-47d8-9012-095629a5ea4d`, name "Manifest", layout `token`, type line "Creature", 2/2. The image URL and its timestamp query are NOT written anywhere in the repository (they change); only the set code and the number are. Nothing of the image is in the repository. When the image cannot be had (offline, blocked, a wrong code) the board draws a plain face-down card, a card back with "2/2 creature", so the game works offline. The controller's own face-down permanent is the same tile with a "face-down" marker, and the preview panel (hover or click) shows the real card with the marker; a "Turn face up {cost}" button appears on it (in the priority actions of that permanent) when the priority decision offers the option. Opponents see the token image and "Face-down creature N". The log words the events: "<Seat> manifests the top card of their library", "<Seat> turns a face-down card face up (it is <Name>)" (the name appears because the card is public after it); when a face-down permanent leaves, the line names it (CR 708.9).

## Tests, soak, versions

Tests first (the scenario DSL, SYNTHETIC cards in `src/cards/fixtures/synthetic-manifest.ts`), one per rule: layer 1b (2/2 no name, types, colors, abilities, text), later layers on top (counter, pump, control), manifest from the top of a library (the object, the label, controller, the events, no card named, no enters effects, "enters tapped" and counters of the real card do not apply, an enters trigger of another permanent sees a creature), an empty library, a commander card, a token cannot be manifested, `turnFaceUp` (legal and illegal cases, the payment, cancel, no stack, no trigger, keeps counters and damage and summoning sickness, a lord starts to work, creature card with {X} costs, a card without a mana cost, a land and an instant cannot, priority with the stack busy, in another player's turn, special action does not use the stack), leaving (revealed, new object, counters gone, LKI), copying (both directions, CR 708.10), phasing, control change (the view follows the controller), transform refusal, hidden information (every view field and every event kind, the stack, targets, trigger order, `determinize` agreeing with the view and not equal to the real card), Reality Shift end to end. Grammar tests: examples, rejects, a lowering test, a test that the engine runs what the lowering gives. AI: handler tests (turns up when worth it, never a non-creature, never peeks) and the soak of the 'manifest' deck style (SYNTHETIC cards only), whose `replayGame` gives the same final state. Mutation checks on the engine rules at the end. `ENGINE_VERSION`, `PARSER_VERSION` and the derive digests are not touched here; the integrator bumps and records them once (the tests that fail only because of a version or digest are listed in the report).
