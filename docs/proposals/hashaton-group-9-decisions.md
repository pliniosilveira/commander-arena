# Hashaton deck, group 9: decisions (issue 31)

Rona, Herald of Invasion // Rona, Tolarian Obliterator. The numbers are provisional (D-1180 to D-1185 in code comments and docs); the integrator renumbers them into `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## D-1180: A Phyrexian mana symbol in the cost of an ordinary activated ability (2026-11-13)
- Context: the front face of Rona has `{5}{B/P}: Transform ~. Activate only as a sorcery.`, an ordinary activated ability (it adds no mana, so it uses the stack, CR 605.1a), whose cost has a Phyrexian symbol (CR 107.4f: one mana of its color or 2 life). The parser read `{5}` with `cost.mana` and stopped at `{B/P}`. The engine already pays such a cost: the mana planner resolves a Phyrexian symbol into the mana or 2 life (`mana/payment.ts`, least life first, never down to 0 life), and an activation's mana goes through the same `payMana` decision as a spell. No scenario test had an activated ability with the symbol, so it was proven first (`scenario-xx-hashaton-g9-phyrexian.test.ts`): with a black source the planner pays `{B}`, with none it pays 2 life, paying by hand with no taps pays the life even when a Swamp is untapped, 3 life is enough and 2 is not (the planner keeps 1 life), and the activation is offered or not by the same rules. It needed no engine change.
- Decision: a rule `cost.mana-phyrexian` (`rules/cost/hashaton-group-9.ts`) reads plain symbols and single-color Phyrexian symbols (`{5}{B/P}`, `{B/P}`, `{1}{U/P}{U/P}`), at least one Phyrexian, and writes the `mana` cost with the symbols as they are (`['5', 'B/P']`). It is registered after the hybrid and X rules and before `cost.mana`. `lowerCost` refuses a Phyrexian symbol in the cost of a mana ability (`options.mana`: the engine's `manaAbilityCostKinds` takes generic, colored and hybrid mana only, `supported.ts`). Keyword costs (Cycling, Equip, Ward) are read by `cost.mana` by name, which does not read the symbol, and `lowerCost` refuses any `/` symbol without `options.hybrid`, so an additional cost and a keyword's cost keep it unparsed. The callers that allow hybrid symbols and pay through the planner (an ordinary activation, the tax of "can't attack you unless their controller pays {W/P}", `lowerPlayRule`) take it.
- Diagnostics: the corpus line "{G/P}, {T}: Add {G}{G}." of `convert-grammar-corpus.test.ts` ("still unread: a Phyrexian symbol in a mana ability cost") is still unsupported, but its closest rule is now `cost.mana-phyrexian` with the reason `notRunnable` (the cost reads and the mana ability refuses it), so the expectation was updated; no golden card changed because of it.
- Alternatives: extending `cost.mana` (it is shared by the keyword costs and a Phyrexian symbol would be read by them, then dropped by the lowering with a less exact reason); lowering a Phyrexian symbol in a mana ability's cost (the planner of a mana ability's own cost has no life source for it).
- Consequences: every ordinary activation with a Phyrexian symbol reads (the transform of Rona, and others with the same shape). A hybrid Phyrexian symbol (`{W/U/P}`), a Phyrexian symbol with a hybrid, X or snow symbol, and `{P}` stay unread. Limit: the planner keeps 1 life, so a player at 2 life is not offered a cost paid with 2 life (CR 119.4 allows it); this is the engine's existing choice for automatic payment.

## D-1181: "Whenever a source deals damage to ~" (2026-11-13)
- Context: the engine's `dealtDamage` trigger (D-133) fires once for each source that damages the permanent, in the moment the damage is dealt (CR 120.3, 603.2c, 603.2g) and carries `triggerPlayer`, the controller of the source (last-known information when the source is gone, CR 113.7a). The only phrase was "~ is dealt damage".
- Decision: a rule `trigger.source-deals-damage` (`rules/trigger/hashaton-g9.ts`): "a source deals [combat] damage to <subject>", the subject read like `trigger.dealt-damage` (`~`, an attached permanent, a permanent of a kind). It writes the same IR event (`dealtDamage`, `combat`) and adds an `actor`, a noun standing for the controller of the source, which is the antecedent of "that source's controller" in the body (D-1182). `thatPlayerOf` (`lower/triggers.ts`) lowers an event with an actor, now also `dealtDamage`, to the engine's `triggerPlayer`. Not read: a source of a kind ("a creature deals damage to ~", "a source you control deals damage to ~": the engine's trigger has no filter on the source), damage to a player ("deals damage to you"), noncombat damage (another event).
- Alternatives: a new engine trigger with a source filter (no card needs it).
- Consequences: the trigger of the back face reads. Several sources at once trigger once each (CR 603.2c); damage that kills the permanent still triggers; the controller of a spell that has left the stack is still the player (tested with the Bolt already in the graveyard).

## D-1182: "That source's controller" (2026-11-13)
- Context: the body of the trigger of D-1181 names the player by "that source's controller". `noun.its-controller` reads "its controller" and "that creature's controller" (the controller of an object the text named, not the source of damage).
- Decision: a noun rule `noun.that-sources-controller` (`rules/noun/hashaton-g9.ts`) writes an anaphor that the binding pass classifies as a player pronoun (`classify`, `ir/bind.ts`), so it points at the player the trigger names: the actor of D-1181. A text with no such trigger has an unbound reference and is dropped (tested: "Whenever ~ attacks, that source's controller ...").
- Alternatives: reading it as "that player" (the binding of "that player" has other antecedents: a target, a drawn card).
- Consequences: only the effect rule of D-1183 reads it today; another player verb can add the noun to its subjects.

## D-1183: `exileRandomFromHand` (2026-11-13)
- Context: "that source's controller exiles a card from their hand at random". Precedents: `exileRandomFromGraveyard` (D-235, the controller's own graveyard, no player) and `discard` with `random` (D-209, a discard to the graveyard, with the Madness exception). Nothing exiled a random card of a hand, and nothing let the trigger player do it.
- Decision: the effect `{ kind: 'exileRandomFromHand', player: PlayerSelector }` (`rules/exile-random-hand.ts`, called from `effects.ts`). The player the selector names exiles one card of their own hand, chosen with the seeded generator stored in the state (`shuffle`, as `discard` at random and `exileRandomFromGraveyard` do), so a game replays move for move. Nobody is asked. The card is exiled face up (CR 406.3), a new object (CR 400.7) that `moved` names afterwards (CR 400.7j, `moving` starts the list empty and the move adds the card). An empty hand exiles nothing and leaves `moved` empty, so "it" of the instructions after it names nothing (a stale `moved` from an earlier instruction is not read: tested with two exiles in a row). `validateCard` accepts one player (you, a target, the controller or owner of an object, the trigger player, the defending player) and refuses several (`each opponent`: there would be no single "it"); `triggerPlayer` needs a trigger about a player (the existing check). `movesObjects` counts it, so `moved` is valid after it. The feature `effect:exileRandomFromHand` is declared.
- Hidden information: the pick is made inside the engine from the generator, the cards of the hand are not shown to the other seat before it (`getView` shows a count), the exile event and the view show the one card exiled, and nothing else changes. Tested as a two-world audit (two games that differ only in the cards of the hand give the same view of the other seat, the same events and the same position picked, and no name of the hand is in what the other seat is shown before the exile).
- Alternatives: a `discard` that exiles (it would pass through madness and discard triggers, which an exile is not); the player choosing (the card says "at random").
- Consequences: Rona's back face and any "target player exiles a card from their hand at random" read once the grammar adds the subject. Web wording: "That player exiles a card from their hand at random".

## D-1184: The offers after the exile: "put it onto the battlefield" and "cast it without paying its mana cost" (2026-11-13)
- Context: "If it's a land card, you may put it onto the battlefield under your control. Otherwise, you may cast it without paying its mana cost." The engine has every piece (D-1183 `moved`; `conditional` with `objectMatches` of `moved` and an `else`; `may`; `moveToZone` with `controller: 'you'`; `castFree` over `moved`, D-145). The grammar had no rule for "put it onto the battlefield" (only the cards of a graveyard and of a hand) or "cast it without paying its mana cost" (only a spell of a hand or the card a linked ability exiled), and the generic "you may" of a triggered ability is one question for the whole effect, which cannot sit beside a condition or an `else`.
- Decision: the clause rules `effect.put-it-onto-battlefield` ("put it onto the battlefield [under your control]", verb `putMovedOntoBattlefield`) and `effect.cast-it-free` ("cast it without paying its mana cost", verb `castMovedFree`), and the clause rule `effect.exile-random-from-hand` (verb `exileRandomFromHand`, the subjects "that source's controller" and "that player"). The binding pass makes "it" after an `exileRandomFromHand` clause the card it exiled (`ref: 'moved'`, as `putExiledWith` does). Both offer lowerings accept an optional clause (`acceptsOptional`): "you may put it" is the engine's `may` of the move, and "you may cast it" is the question `castFree` asks itself (a cast with no "you may" would be mandatory, which the engine does not run, so it is not lowered; the land with no "you may" is a plain move). "It" is only the exiled card: after any other instruction the clause is not lowered. A triggered ability keeps the "you may" of those two verbs on the clause (`asksItself`, `lower/triggers.ts`) instead of making it the ability's one question. In `lowerStep` the sentence "Otherwise, ..." no longer refuses an optional clause outright; `lowerClauses` decides (an optional clause lowers only through a lowering with `acceptsOptional`, so "Otherwise, you may draw a card" is still dropped). The conditional sentence "If it's a land card, ..." is the existing `condition.it-matches` read against `moved`. The three clause rules and the noun rule are `quietly` registered: a line they do not read ("Put a +1/+1 counter on ~", "Put any number of cards from your hand on the bottom of your library ...") must keep stopping where it always did, so the shared words "put " and "cast " do not become the closest rule of its diagnostic (two corpus lines showed it).
- Alternatives: one rule that reads the three sentences of Rona (the shape of D-229's Hofri rule): rejected because the pieces are the general ones ("it" after an exile, a land or a free cast); the generic `may` of the trigger with the land question folded in (the engine's `optional` covers the whole effect and cannot sit beside a condition).
- Consequences: the whole text of Rona, Tolarian Obliterator lowers to the same AST the SYNTHETIC engine card defines (`synthetic-hashaton-g9.ts`). A creature or another permanent with the cast is put onto the battlefield by the resolution of the spell under the control of the player who cast it (CR 608.3a), so the card is Rona's controller's, owned by the opponent. A spell with targets asks Rona's controller for them (CR 601.2c). A card that cannot be cast (no legal target) is not offered. Web: the offers read "you may put it onto the battlefield under your control" and "you may cast it without paying its mana cost".

## D-1185: Wording in the web (2026-11-13)
- Context: the chooser and the stack gist said nothing of a transform or the exile.
- Decision: `ability-label.ts` words `transform` ("Transform this creature") and `exileRandomFromHand` ("That player exiles a card from their hand at random", "You exile a card from your hand at random"), and a move of the card an instruction exiled to the battlefield under your control reads "Put it onto the battlefield under your control" (not "Return it", which says it was there before). The Phyrexian symbol prints as it is, `{5}{B/P}`.
- Consequences: the activation shows as "{5}{B/P}: Transform this creature (sorcery speed)"; the trigger on the stack reads "That player exiles a card from their hand at random. If it's a land card, you may put it onto the battlefield under your control. Otherwise, you may cast it without paying its mana cost". The question of the land (`confirmOptional`) is worded as every `may`: the source name, its text and "do it?".
