# Hashaton deck (issue 31), group 10: Restless Dreams. Decisions (provisional numbers D-1190 to D-1193)

Restless Dreams: "As an additional cost to cast this spell, discard X cards. Return X target creature cards from your graveyard to your hand." (the real card, an acceptance card of the golden corpus; its text carries no flashback). The decisions below have provisional numbers; the integrator renumbers them into `docs/DECISIONS.md`. The rulings of the card were not fetched (Scryfall is blocked in a cloud session): what follows rests on the CR text of the owner's rules repository and is marked where the card's own rulings would matter.

## D-1190: "discard X cards" is an additional cost announced like "pay X life" (2026-11-13)
- Context: the engine announced X only for an {X} in the mana cost and for "pay X life" (D-103, `XCost { mana, life, loyalty }`). Restless Dreams has neither: its X is the number of cards discarded as an additional cost, and the text says nothing else about X (CR 107.3a: an X in an additional cost is chosen and announced as the spell is cast; while the spell is on the stack every X in its costs equals the announced value).
- Decision: a new `Cost` kind `{ kind: 'discardX' }`, allowed only in `CardFace.additionalCosts` (once, and not beside a fixed `discard` cost: `validateCard` refuses both, as it refuses the kind in the cost of an activated ability, in a choice of additional costs and in `unsupportedFeatureIssues`). `XCost` gets `discard?: number` (cards for each point of X; `spellXCost` counts the `discardX` costs of the face, whichever way the spell is cast, so a flashback cast announces X too, CR 702.34a: the additional costs apply, CR 118.9d), `hasX` counts it, `ChooseXDecision.perX.discard` carries it, and `spellCost` adds `perX.discard * x` to `SpellCost.discard`. The existing machinery pays it: after the mana, `payAdditionalCosts` discards exactly that many (all of the hand when it holds exactly that many, otherwise the `chooseCards` decision with `forCost`, `min` = `max` = X); `cancel` rewinds the cast; the cards are public (`discarded` events, `reveal: true`) and trigger "whenever you discard" (CR 701.9a). `canPaySpellCost` already refuses a discard larger than the hand without the spell, so `largestSpellX` is limited by the hand with no new code; with an {X} in the mana cost as well the same X is both (the largest value that both can pay).
- Alternatives: a `payX` generalisation (`{ kind: 'payX', resource: 'life' | 'cards' }`) was rejected, it would have changed every `payXLife` card and test for no gain. Reusing `discard` with a count of `{ kind: 'x' }` was rejected: `Cost.discard.count` is a number everywhere (Ward, activated abilities, the web), and an X there would need every reader to know about the announcement.
- Consequences: "discard X cards" works with "pay X life" and an {X} in the mana cost on one card (each point of X is all three). Not built: "discard X cards" as the cost of an activated ability (the engine does not announce X for a `discardX`; `docs/ROADMAP.md`), "discard X cards at random", "discard X creature cards".

## D-1191: a target slot with as many targets as X, and the order of the announcement (2026-11-13)
- Context: "Return X target creature cards from your graveyard" has a number of targets that is not fixed by the text but by the announced X (CR 601.2c: "In some cases, the number of targets will be defined by the spell's text", and once determined it doesn't change). CR 601.2b announces X before 601.2c chooses the targets, and 601.2h pays the costs after both (the discard comes with the other costs, after the mana), so a card discarded as the cost was not in the graveyard when the targets were chosen: it is not a target. (The ruling of the card, as far as it is remembered, says the same; it could not be fetched, so this rests on the CR order.)
- Decision: `TargetSpec.countX?: true` (`selector:countX` in `SUPPORTED_FEATURES`): the slot takes exactly X targets. `targetBounds` says `{ min: 0, max: unlimited }` for it (so `canCast` and `targetsPossible` see no obstacle: X = 0 is always a legal announcement); `targetChoiceSpecs` takes the announced X and fixes `min` = `max` = X; `maxTargetCountX` limits the X that `toX` offers to the legal targets of every such slot (the decision's `max` is the smaller of that and `largestSpellX`); with X = 0 `toTargets` leaves the slot out (a spell with no target for it, which resolves and does nothing: `fizzled` needs a chosen target, CR 608.2b); `copyDecision` gives a copy the X of its original (`StackData.x` is copied, CR 707.10) so a copy that may choose new targets must choose exactly as many. `validateCard` allows `countX` only in a slot of a spell's own text (not an ability, a trigger or a mode: `declareTargets` takes `countXAllowed`) of a face with `spell.x: true`, on an object (never a player), with no `min`, `max`, `divide`, `anyNumber`, `onePerOpponent`, `upToThatMany`, kicker or gift condition. A target that left the graveyard before resolution is skipped as for any slot (CR 608.2b). `legalActionsForDecision` lists the combinations of a long graveyard up to `MAX_LISTED_CHOICES` (`firstCombinations`), as it does for a choice among many permanents; any legal answer is accepted.
- Alternatives: asking X after the targets was rejected (the targets depend on X, and CR 601.2b says X first). A `Amount`-valued `min`/`max` on every slot was rejected as a wider change than this one card needs.
- Consequences: X is offered up to the smaller of the cards in hand (besides the spell) and the legal graveyard creature cards (and the mana, when the cost has an {X}); the spell is castable with X = 0 whatever the graveyard holds. A 0/0 or a bigger X than the graveyard is never reached.

## D-1192: the grammar of Restless Dreams (2026-11-13)
- Context: the two sentences were `unrecognised`.
- Decision: the cost rule `cost.discard-x` ("Discard X cards", `parser/rules/cost/hashaton-group-10.ts`, read by `static.additional-cost` only, so Cycling, Ward and the costs of abilities never read it; `COST_DISCARD` keeps rejecting "Discard X cards"), the noun rule `noun.x-target-graveyard-cards` ("X target creature cards from your graveyard", `parser/rules/noun/hashaton-group-10.ts`: your own graveyard only, the plural phrase of `noun.target-graveyard-card`, with the IR count `{ kind: 'x' }`; read by `effect.return` only, through its own operand list). The lowering: `lowerCost` gives `discardX` only with `CostOptions.discardX` (an additional cost of a spell, `lower/spell-costs.ts`, which also asks for `ability:xCost` as for "pay X life" and refuses a second discard cost), and `declareTarget` (`lower/nouns.ts`) gives `countX` only in the text of a spell, for object targets of a graveyard or the battlefield, marks the context as reading X (`ctx.usesX`, so the face gets `spell.x: true` and the engine's X check sees a cost that announces it) and needs `selector:countX`. `FeatureRef`s: `cost:discardX`, `selector:countX`, `ability:xCost`. Anything else that says "X target" stays unread or unsupported: "up to X", "X target creature card", another graveyard, "discard X cards" with a fixed `discard`, "X target" in an ability or a trigger.
- Alternatives: adding "X target" to the shared `targetOf` of the noun family was rejected: it would have made every `noun.target-*` rule read "X target ..." and changed the reasons reported for many cards that stay unsupported.
- Consequences: a spell whose text reads X without an X in a cost is refused by the engine's own check, not by the grammar (`validateCard`: "spell uses X but its cost has no {X} and no ..."); the import test covers it.

## D-1193: the CasualAgent and the web for "discard X cards" (2026-11-13)
- Context: every consumer of `XCost`: the web prompt, the preview, the cost text and the CasualAgent's choice of X and of whether to cast.
- Decision: web: `perXText`/`xPrompt` say "Discard X cards; you can choose up to X = N" when the cost is only cards (the engine's `max` already holds the hand and the legal targets), "Each X costs {1}, and you discard X cards" beside mana or life, `xCostAt` adds "discard N cards", `activationCostPart` prints a `discardX` as "Discard X cards" (the preview's additional cost and the hover), and the preview of a spell on the stack says how many cards were discarded. AI: `chooseXAnswer` takes no more X than the cards it is glad to lose (`expendableCount`, a count of the surplus lands and uncastable cards, the two tests of `expendable`), `additionalCostsWorthPaying` casts a spell with a `discardX` only with at least one such card and, for "return X target creature cards from your graveyard", a creature card to return, and `worthCasting` no longer asks for an opposing creature for it (the regex of "target creature" matched the text and the agent would never have cast the card). The targets are the first cards listed (any legal answer; a smarter pick is left to the heuristic agents). Hidden information: the number of cards discarded and the cards are public, the choice before it is not (`waitingOn` names the discard).
- Alternatives: an X of the whole hand was rejected (the agent would discard cards it wants). A change of `worthCasting` for every "target creature card from your graveyard" spell (Raise Dead) was rejected as a wider change to the agent than this card needs; it is listed in `docs/ROADMAP.md`.
- Consequences: the soak style `hashatonDiscardX` (`packages/ai/test/hashaton-g10-games.test.ts`) plays the cards between random and casual agents and restates the rules of the slice from the events.
