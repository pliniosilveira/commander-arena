# Gisa deck, group 2: provisional decisions D-1410 to D-1417

Provisional numbers for the lead to fold into `docs/DECISIONS.md` (issue 41; group 2: crimes, tokens and dying creatures; cards Gisa, the Hellraiser, Forsaken Miner, Mirkwood Bats and Morbid Opportunist). The format is that of `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## D-1410: Committing a crime: the event `crimeCommitted` and the trigger kind `crimeCommitted` (CR 700.13) (2026-11-14)

- Context: "Whenever you commit a crime" (Gisa, Forsaken Miner) needs the engine to know when a player commits one. CR 700.13: a player commits a crime as they cast a spell, activate an ability, or put a triggered ability on the stack and that spell or ability targets at least one opponent; at least one permanent, spell, or ability an opponent controls; and/or at least one card in an opponent's graveyard. The engine already had `becameTarget` (objects only: a player is no object, so it is no help for "targets an opponent") at the three places where a spell, an ability or a trigger goes on the stack with its targets chosen.
- Decision: a new event `crimeCommitted { seat, object }` (`state/events.ts`), made by `rules/crime.ts` (`crimeEvents`, `isCrime`) right after the `becameTarget` events of the same spell or ability in `cast.ts` (`finishCast`), `activate.ts` (`finishActivation`) and `trigger-stack.ts` (`targetsChosen`), once for the object however many targets qualify. A target qualifies when it is a player other than `seat`, a permanent whose controller (read with `controllerOf`, D-189) is not `seat`, a spell or ability on the stack whose controller is not `seat`, or a card in a graveyard whose owner is not `seat` (an opponent is any other player, CR 102.2; a card in an opponent's hand, library or exile is no crime). A copy of a spell is put on the stack, not cast, so `copy-spell.ts` makes none; a target changed afterwards (CR 115.7) is not a new casting and makes none. The trigger kind `crimeCommitted { who: 'you' | 'opponent' | 'any' }` (`ast/types.ts`, listed in `SUPPORTED_FEATURES`, validated, `triggerHasPlayer`: the player who committed it is `triggerPlayer`, the spell or ability `triggerSubject`) reads it. Because the event is made as the targets are chosen, the trigger waits for the next round and goes on the stack above the spell or ability, as ward's does (`PendingTrigger.deferred`).
- Alternatives: a `TurnLog.crimes` list (the cards that ask "if you've committed a crime this turn" are not in this deck: left to the first card that needs it, ROADMAP); computing the crime from the `spellCast` and `abilityActivated` events in `triggers.ts` (it would need the targets of triggered abilities, chosen later, and a second reading of the same rule); extending `becameTarget` to players (it would trigger ward-like abilities that read a permanent, and nothing needs a player's becoming a target).
- Consequences: a crime by the opponent is a crime for `who: 'opponent'` and `'any'` too. The four effects of the group need nothing else. The web has a log line and a trigger wording for it (D-1415). `docs/RULES_COVERAGE.md` has the rule.

## D-1411: "Whenever you create a token": the trigger kind `tokenCreated`, and "create or sacrifice a token" as two abilities (CR 111.2, 701.21, 603.2c) (2026-11-14)

- Context: Mirkwood Bats, "Whenever you create or sacrifice a token, each opponent loses 1 life." The engine read "you create one or more creature tokens" as a batch of tokens that enter under your control (`entersBatch`, D-229), which says nothing of one token and counts the tokens a player gets control of, not those a player creates. The event `tokenCreated { seat }` (the player who creates the token: its owner and the player it enters under, CR 111.2) already exists.
- Decision: a trigger kind `tokenCreated { who }`: one occurrence for each token created by the player `who` names (relative to the controller of the ability), `triggerSubject` the token, `triggerPlayer` its creator. A copy of a permanent spell that becomes a token (CR 707.10f) is not created by an effect and has no `tokenCreated` event, so it does not count. "Create or sacrifice a token" is the two engine abilities of an "A or B" trigger with one body (CR 603.2c): `tokenCreated` and `permanentSacrificed` over `{ token: true }` (the sacrifice trigger already looks back at the permanent as it was). The parser rule `trigger.create-token` reads "you create a token", "an opponent creates a token", "a player creates a token" and, for the player who can also sacrifice in the engine (`you` and any player), "creates or sacrifices a token"; a kind of token ("a creature token", "a Treasure token") and several tokens are not read ("one or more tokens" stays `trigger.you-create-tokens`).
- Alternatives: reading "create a token" as `entersBattlefield` of `{ token: true, controller: 'you' }` (an Oracle card that puts a token onto the battlefield under your control that an opponent created would be a different event; and a token that changes controllers would count differently); one ability with two events inside the engine's trigger (the engine's one-event-one-ability form is that of `trigger.or`, D-111, and the sacrifice trigger needs a look-back that the other kind does not).
- Consequences: a token maker that creates two tokens triggers Mirkwood Bats twice. This follows CR 603.2c (one occurrence for each event, and each token is created by its own event in `createTokens`); the ruling of the card on Scryfall was not fetched (blocked in a cloud session) and is marked unverified. "That player" in the body of the opponent's form is `triggerPlayer`.

## D-1412: "Whenever one or more other creatures die": a batch of the creatures of every player (CR 700.4, 603.10a, 603.2c) (2026-11-14)

- Context: Morbid Opportunist, "Whenever one or more other creatures die, draw a card. This ability triggers only once each turn." The batch trigger `diesBatch` and its parser rule `trigger.dies-batch` (D-206) read the permanents of the controller only ("... you control die").
- Decision: the rule also reads a group with no controller ("one or more creatures die", "one or more other creatures die", "one or more other creatures and/or artifacts die"): the engine's `diesBatch` over the filter, whose controller is left out, so the creatures of every player count, as they were on the battlefield. The group of "an opponent controls" is still not read. The `once each turn` limit is the engine's `oncePerTurn` of D-149 (it counts the triggering, so a second batch the same turn does nothing), and the creature itself dying with others is seen by its own ability (CR 603.10a), but its own death alone is not (the filter says "other").
- Alternatives: none; the engine needed no change (`scenario-xx-gisa-g2-tokens-dies.test.ts` proves it).
- Consequences: the wording of the web (`game/trigger-text.ts`, `diesBatch`) said it already.

## D-1413: "Ward—{2}, Pay 2 life." (CR 702.21a, 118.1) (2026-11-14)

- Context: Gisa's ward has a mana cost and a payment of life. The keyword node carries a list of costs, the engine pays the life and then the mana as the ward ability resolves (`rules/pay-unless.ts`: the payer is asked once, the life is paid, then the mana), and `validateCard` allows both. Only the single-cost forms were read.
- Decision: `keyword.ward` also reads the dash form of a mana cost, a comma and a payment of life ("Ward—{2}, Pay 2 life."), as `keyword.equip` reads "Equip—{2}, Pay 2 life." (D-176); `lowerEquipCosts` is shared (parameter `name`). The rule has no `leading` word (the line starts with the cost's words, as the equip rule's), so it is tried at every keyword line and fails at its first literal. Not read: a sacrifice or a discard beside the mana, two payments of life, a zero payment, a period after the plain form.
- Alternatives: a general list of ward costs (ward with three costs does not exist on a card, and each cost needs its own question when the payer has to choose).
- Consequences: a ward payer who cannot pay both is not asked (CR 118.3: `canPayUnless` checks the life and the mana together); the web asks "Pay {2} and 2 life or X is countered?" (`wardPrompt` already read a list of costs).

## D-1414: "Skeletons and Zombies you control" (CR 205.3m, 611.3a) (2026-11-14)

- Context: Gisa's static "Skeletons and Zombies you control get +1/+1 and have menace." has two creature types in the plural with one "you control". `noun.subtype-union` reads "A you control and other B you control" (two controllers); `noun.plain-permanents` read the first word and stopped at "and".
- Decision: the noun rule `noun.subtype-pair` (`rules/noun/gisa-g2.ts`), tried before `noun.plain-permanents` and listed among the subjects of `static.permanents`: two different creature types, each in the plural, joined by "and", then "you control"; the engine's `anyOf` of the two types with `controller: 'you'` on the group. "Other", a third type, a singular, a word that is no creature type, another controller and no controller are not read. The web says the group as "Skeletons and Zombies you control" (`groupPhrase`, `game/static-text.ts`), where it said "Skeleton permanents you control and Zombie permanents you control".
- Alternatives: extending `noun.subtype-union` (two shapes in one rule, and a merge conflict with the Zombie group of this deck, which works in the same noun rules).
- Consequences: other cards that say two types ("Elves and Wolves you control") are read too.

## D-1415: Wording in the web (2026-11-14)

- Context: the engine gets one trigger kind, one event, a group and a ward cost.
- Decision: `game/trigger-text.ts` says "Whenever you commit a crime" and "Whenever you create a token" (for `you`, `an opponent`, `a player`) and a token filter of no kind as "a token" ("Whenever you sacrifice a token", not "a permanent token"); `game/describe.ts` has the log line "You commit a crime with Lightning Bolt."
