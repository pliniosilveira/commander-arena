# Hashaton deck, group 6: Serra's Emissary (provisional decisions D-1170 and D-1171)

The numbers are provisional. The integrator renumbers them into `docs/DECISIONS.md` and replaces `D-1170`, `D-1171` in the code comments and the documents.

## D-1170: "As this creature enters, choose a card type", and protection from a card type (2026-11-13)
- Context: Serra's Emissary (the Hashaton deck, issue 31) says "As this creature enters, choose a card type." and "You and creatures you control have protection from the chosen card type." The engine knew three choices as a permanent enters (a creature type, a color, a name from a list the card gives, D-100, D-151) and protection from colors only (D-229, D-259: `protectedColors` and `sourceColors` of `rules/protection.ts`).
- Decision: a fourth choice, `asEntersChoose` with `what: 'cardType'`: the controller chooses one of the nine card types of `CARD_TYPES` (artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, instant, kindred, land, planeswalker, sorcery: CR 205.2a lists more types, among them Dungeon, Plane and Scheme, which the engine does not model and no Commander deck plays). The decision is a `chooseOption` of the new purpose `cardType`, the answer is `GameObject.chosen.cardType`, the event is an `optionChosen` of that purpose. Protection from a card type is two keyword qualities: `{ kind: 'protection', from: 'cardType', types }` (named types) and `{ kind: 'protection', from: 'chosenCardType' }` (the card type the permanent with the ability chose; linked abilities, CR 607.2d, so `validateCard` requires the card to ask the question). A static `grantKeyword` of `chosenCardType` is resolved to `cardType` as the grant is applied (`rules/derive-statics.ts`), as D-259 does for the commander's colors, and gives nothing when its permanent chose nothing. `rules/protection.ts` reads colors and card types alike: `sourceTypes` (a permanent as it is, a spell or a card in another zone with its card types, an ability's source, and last-known information for an object that left, CR 113.7a), `protectedTypes`, and `hasProtectionFrom` is true when the source has a color or any card type the object is protected from (CR 702.16a: a source with several types, an artifact creature, matches when any of them does). The four consumers (targeting by `canBeTargetedBy`, damage in `dealDamageAsking`, `canAttach` for Auras and Equipment and their state-based unattaching, and the block legality) read `hasProtectionFrom` and so need no change of their own. The AI (`casual-card-type.ts`) chooses the card type most of the opponents' permanents have (a land is not counted, ties go to the type listed first, `creature` when there is nothing to count). The web asks it with a button for each type.
- Alternatives: a `chosen.option` of the existing `option` question (no engine change, but the nine names would be strings the engine knows nothing about, and `protection` could not read them as types); the type chosen as a creature type (wrong: the card type is not a subtype); a protection that names the chosen type through the filter machinery (`chosenType` of an `ObjectFilter`): protection works on the characteristic values of a source, not on a set of permanents.
- Consequences: any later "protection from [card type]" (printed or granted), from an artifact, a creature or a named type, needs only a grammar rule. Unverified: no ruling of Serra's Emissary could be fetched (the cloud sandbox blocks Scryfall); none is assumed beyond the CR text of 702.16.
- A bug found on the way and fixed (`rules/derive-layers.ts` `hasAbility`): a keyword that an object already has is not granted again, and the comparison was by the kind of the keyword, which is right for keywords without parameters and wrong for protection: a creature that had one protection kept only the first it was given from another source, so two Emissaries (or an Emissary and a Plate of the commander's colors) gave one protection, not two (CR 702.16g, 702.16m: protection from different qualities are separate abilities, and only the same quality twice is redundant). Protection is now compared as written. This changes the derived abilities of an object with protection from two sources, so `ENGINE_VERSION` should be bumped by the integrator.

## D-1171: "You have protection from ..." for a player (2026-11-13)
- Context: "You and creatures you control have protection from the chosen card type" gives the protection to the controller as well. The engine had `playerHexproof` ("You have hexproof", D-149), read for targeting only and without the source of the spell or ability.
- Decision: a static `{ kind: 'playerProtection', from: 'chosenCardType' }` (or `from: 'cardType', types`). `rules/player-protection.ts` reads it from the abilities of the permanents the player controls after the layers (`playerProtectedTypes`, `playerHasProtectionFrom`; a permanent that lost its abilities protects nobody, and one that chose nothing protects from nothing). `playerCanBeTargetedBy` takes the source of the spell or ability (a spell or ability of that card type can't target the player, CR 702.16b, even one the player controls), and `dealDamageAsking` prevents damage dealt to a player by a source of the type (CR 702.16e) before the other prevention effects, as it does for a permanent. The sentence lowers to two abilities: the player's static and a `grantKeyword` to "creatures you control" (a statement with the subject "you" and an `also` part). The static ends with its permanent (CR 604.1), and each permanent with the ability has its own choice, so two of them protect from two types.
- Alternatives: one static with a subject that names both the player and the creatures (the AST's static subjects are permanents; the player is not one); reading the protection of a player from the creatures they control (it would be the same choice read twice and would make "creatures you control" and "you" disagree when one of them loses the keyword).
- Consequences: not modelled, and listed in `docs/ROADMAP.md`: an Aura that enchants a player (Curses: the engine's Auras enchant permanents only, so CR 702.16c for a player has nothing to apply to), and damage that a redirection effect makes land on a protected permanent or player (protection is checked for the recipient the damage would be dealt to, before the replacement effects, as D-229 does). Protection of a player from a color, from a player or from everything is not read (the card needs none).
