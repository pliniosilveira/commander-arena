# Niv-Mizzet deck, group 3-c: decisions (provisional numbers)

Decisions of the builder of group 3-c (Quistis Trepe), written with provisional numbers D-990 to D-994 (the integrator renumbers them into `docs/DECISIONS.md`). The code, the tests and the other docs cite these numbers.

## D-990: Casting a target card from a graveyard as an effect resolves: the effect `castCard` and the cast during resolution that pays the mana cost (2026-11-12)
- Context: Quistis Trepe ("When ~ enters, you may cast target instant or sorcery card from a graveyard, ...") lets its controller cast a card of any player's graveyard while the trigger resolves (CR 608.2g), paying its mana cost. The engine's casts during a resolution were all free or for an alternative cost of the card (`castFree`, Cascade, Suspend, Madness, Miracle) and only from exile or the hand; casting from a graveyard needed a standing permission (`mayCast`, flashback, D-232), never a one-time effect on a target, and `beginCast` with `resolving` had no way to pay the ordinary cost.
- Decision: A new effect `castCard { object: target, anyManaType?, exileInstead? }` (`ast/types.ts`, `ast/features.ts`, `ast/validate-parts.ts`: the card is a target of the ability; the riders are `true` when present). As the effect runs (`rules/effects.ts`) the target is read from the targets still legal (CR 608.2b: a card that left its graveyard is an illegal target, so the ability loses it or does nothing) and an instruction `castCard` is queued in two stages like `castExiled` (`rules/cast-card.ts`, dispatched from `rules/stack.ts` because it needs `legality.ts`): `start` asks `canCastDuringResolution(state, controller, card, 'normal', anyManaType)` and goes on to `ask` only when the card could be cast now; `ask` is the existing decision kind `confirmOptional` with a new purpose `castCard` (the card as `subject`, public because it is in a graveyard), so no new decision kind. The answer is routed in `api.ts` `answerConfirm` to `rules/cast-card-answer.ts`: accepting calls `beginCast(state, controller, card, undefined, 0, true, false, riders)`, declining leaves the card in its graveyard. `canCastDuringResolution` gets the alternative `normal` (the card is in a graveyard, whoever owns it: the effect names it; no timing, X as the cast announces it; the cost is the mana cost with no alternative; the check reads the cost with `anyType` when the effect says so); `beginCast` with `resolving` and no alternative reads it as `normal`, takes the zone of the card (a graveyard now, besides the hand and exile), does not use up the standing graveyard permission of D-232 (`graveyardUse` only when not resolving), and writes the riders on the spell's `StackData`. The caster controls the spell and pays; the card keeps its owner (CR 108.3). The cast can be taken back with `cancel` as the others (CR 733: the state at the question is the snapshot).
- Alternatives: Reusing `castFree` with a `paysCost` flag was rejected (its question is a `chooseCards` among a set the effect names, with lands and a hand filter; here the card is a single target and the question is a yes or no). Reusing `mayCast` ("you may cast ... this turn") then letting the ordinary cast action do it was rejected: the cast must happen as the trigger resolves (CR 608.2g), before any player has priority, and the permission would let the player cast it later or not at all, and would leave the card in the graveyard for anyone. A new decision kind was rejected by the task (and is unnecessary: the existing `confirmOptional` with a purpose carries everything). Folding "you may" into an ability-level `optional` plus a plain mandatory cast was rejected: it would ask twice (the ability's question, then the cast's), and the effect has to be optional itself for the cases that are not triggers.
- Consequences: Another card that lets a player cast a card of a graveyard when an effect resolves needs a parser rule and perhaps one more flag, not an engine change. The cast from a graveyard during resolution is never a land (the `canCastDuringResolution` check refuses a land) and never a card of another zone with this effect (the exile and the hand keep `castFree` and `castExiled`). Quistis's rulings could not be fetched (the host is blocked): nothing beyond the CR text is assumed.

## D-991: "Mana of any type can be spent to cast that spell": `SpendTarget.anyType` and `StackData.anyManaType` (2026-11-12)
- Context: CR 609.4b: an effect that says mana of any type can be spent "affects only how the player may pay a cost. It doesn't change that cost, and it doesn't change what mana was actually spent to pay that cost". The engine had no way to let a spell be paid with mana that is not of the colors of its symbols (a restriction on the mana was the only rule that changed what could pay).
- Decision: `SpendTarget` (the description of what the mana is spent on, `mana/pool.ts`) gets `anyType?: true` for a spell. The payment planner (`mana/payment.ts` `buildProfiles`) folds, in each profile, every demand for a color or for colorless into the generic demand when the target has it, so any mana pays any symbol: the colored symbols, `{C}`, the colored half of a hybrid symbol (CR 107.4e: the symbol is then paid by one mana of any type) and the mana option of a Phyrexian symbol (CR 107.4f; the life option is unchanged). The snow demand (`{S}`, CR 107.4h) stays: it needs mana from a snow source, which is not the type of the mana. A restriction on the mana itself (CR 106.6) stays: `restrictionAllows` is not touched. The plan still records what was actually spent (`spentTypes`, `spentSnow`), so "the amount of mana spent" and the mana-from-a-source readers see the real mana (CR 609.4b). The flag is set where the spell's mana is checked and where it is paid: `CostRequest.anyType` and `SpellCost.anyType` (the check that the card can be cast, `canPayRequest`, `canPaySpellCost`), `castManaPlan(..., anyType = spell.stack?.anyManaType)` (automatic payment, the payment options of Convoke and the like), `answerPayment` (the payment by hand, `payByTapping` with the target). `StackData.anyManaType` is written on the spell by `beginCast` from the riders of the effect and is read from the spell on the stack afterwards; it belongs to that one spell (a copy has none, a spell of the same card cast later has none).
- Alternatives: Rewriting the cost symbols to generic before planning was rejected: the cost is shown to the player as printed (the payment prompt, the log, the view's `totalCost`), and a Phyrexian or hybrid symbol is not generic. Giving every mana object a type override was rejected: the permission is the spell's, not the mana's. Folding in `buildItems` (making each source produce `C`) was rejected: it would change what is recorded as spent.
- Consequences: Another effect of this family ("you may spend mana as though it were mana of any color to cast spells of ...", Mycosynth Lattice, Chromatic Orrery) can set the same flag on the target (a static ability is the next step: it would be read in `castManaPlan`). The permission is for spells only; an ability cost is not given it.

## D-992: "If that spell would be put into a graveyard, exile it instead": `StackData.exileInstead` and the replacement in the zone-change pipeline (2026-11-12)
- Context: CR 614.1a, 614.6: a replacement effect that uses "instead" makes the modified event happen, not the original one. The engine has the same shape for Flashback (a spell cast with flashback is exiled when it leaves the stack, `StackData.flashback`), Unearth and finality counters, all in `rules/zones.ts` `moveOne`. Quistis's rider is bound to one spell cast by one effect and applies only to a move to a graveyard.
- Decision: `StackData.exileInstead?: true`, written by `beginCast` from the riders of the effect. In `moveOne`, before the commander replacement and next to the flashback rule: a spell on the stack with the flag that would be put into a graveyard goes to exile instead (resolving as a non-permanent, being countered by an effect or by the rules for illegal targets, any other move to a graveyard); a move to another zone (the battlefield for a permanent spell, hand, library, command) is not changed, and a permanent spell is not affected until it would be put into a graveyard from the stack, which it does not do. The exile is nobody's zone, so `toSeat` is cleared and the card keeps its owner. The flag is on the spell, not the card: the card in exile is a new object with no stack data (CR 400.7), so it is cast again later without the rider.
- Alternatives: Reusing `flashback` as the flag was rejected: it makes the spell cost its flashback cost, appears in the view as a flashback cast and is checked by the invariants against the card's own flashback ability. A continuous effect that names the spell was rejected: the spell is the same object only until it moves, and `StackData` is already where the other per-spell riders live (`free`, `alt`, `hasteUntilEnd`, `graveyardPlay`).
- Consequences: Any card that says "if that spell would be put into a graveyard, exile it instead" (Ephemerate-like riders on spells, Rebound is another mechanism) can set the same flag. Only the move from the stack is replaced.

## D-993: An instant or sorcery that resolves goes to its owner's graveyard (2026-11-12)
- Context: The finishing of a resolution (`rules/stack.ts` `finish`) moved a resolved spell with `toSeat` set to its controller, so a card cast by a player who does not own it ended in the caster's graveyard. CR 400.3: "If an object would go to any library, graveyard, or hand other than its owner's, it goes to its owner's corresponding zone". The two are the same player for every earlier card, since only a card of another graveyard (this effect) is cast by a player who does not own it.
- Decision: the destination seat of a non-permanent spell that resolves is its owner; a permanent still enters under its controller (CR 110.2) and an Adventure is exiled for its caster (CR 715.3d). Found by the first scenario test (the Bolt of an opponent's graveyard went to the caster's graveyard when cast without the exile rider).
- Alternatives: None considered: the CR is explicit. A countered spell already goes to its owner's graveyard (`moveObject` defaults to the owner).
- Consequences: Nothing changes for a card cast from its owner's zones. The same applies to any later effect that lets a player cast another player's card.

## D-994: Reading the text: one sentence with its rider, and the sentence's own "you may" (2026-11-12)
- Context: "Blue Magic — When ~ enters, you may cast target instant or sorcery card from a graveyard, and mana of any type can be spent to cast that spell. If that spell would be put into a graveyard, exile it instead." is a trigger whose body is two sentences, the second of which is a rider of the first, and begins with "you may", which the triggered-ability lowering turns into an `optional` of the whole ability (CR 603.5) with the clause lowered as a plain one. The cast effect asks its own question, so that reading would ask twice. ("Blue Magic —" is an ability word, no rules meaning, CR 207.2c; the flavor-word rule of the parser already takes it off when the line does not read as written.)
- Decision: `parser/segment.ts` joins the sentence "you may cast target ... from a graveyard[, and mana of any type can be spent to cast that spell]." with a following "If that spell would be put into a graveyard, exile it instead." (as it does for other two-sentence instructions). The rule `effect.cast-graveyard-card` (`parser/rules/effect/niv-group-3-c.ts`; leading `cast` and `you`) reads "(you may) cast target <types> card from a graveyard" with the optional riders, only one target and only "a graveyard" (not "your graveyard", not "up to", not several, not "this turn", not "without paying its mana cost"), with nothing joined after it. The lowering (`parser/lower/niv-group-3-c.ts`, `acceptsOptional`) writes `castCard` and refuses a clause that is neither optional (the sentence's "you may") nor says "you may" itself, so a mandatory cast is not read. In `parser/lower/triggers.ts` a sentence of `castCard` clauses that are optional is a permission (like `mayPlay`): the ability gets no `optional` of its own and the clause keeps its optional mark for the lowering. The second sentence is taken into the clause (it is "that spell"), not read alone: "If that spell would be put into a graveyard, exile it instead." as a line of its own is still unread.
- Alternatives: Lowering to a `may` effect around a mandatory cast was rejected (two questions, and the engine's `castCard` is optional by itself). Reading the rider as its own rule was rejected (it needs the spell cast by the first sentence, which no other reading names).
- Consequences: The same lead and riders read for an activated ability when the second sentence is absent; with it the activated and spell routes classify the line as a replacement before the sentences are split (ROADMAP). `PARSER_VERSION` and the golden snapshot are the integrator's: the real card reads as supported with the AST of `docs/MECHANICS.md`.
