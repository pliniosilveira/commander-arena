# Hylda deck, group 1: decisions (issue 42, provisional numbers D-1500 to D-1506)

Group 1 of the Hylda deck: tapping triggers and tap-on-attack effects (Hylda of the Icy Crown, Icewrought Sentry, Sharae of Numbing Depths, Verity Circle, Hammers of Moradin, Elvish Mariner, Pippin, Guard of the Citadel). The lead folds these into `docs/DECISIONS.md` with the next unused numbers; the code and the docs of the group cite `D-1500` to `D-1506`, so the numbers in the code must follow if they change. No decision of the owner is asked.

## D-1500: The `tapped` event says who tapped the permanent and whether it was declared as an attacker; the trigger `becomesTapped` reads both (CR 701.26a, 508.1f, 603.2c)

- Context: "Whenever you tap an untapped creature an opponent controls" (Hylda, Icewrought Sentry, Sharae) and "Whenever a creature an opponent controls becomes tapped, if it isn't being declared as an attacker" (Verity Circle) need two facts about a tap that the engine's `tapped` event did not carry: who tapped the permanent (the controller of the effect that did it, or the player who paid a cost) and whether it became tapped as it was declared as an attacker. The engine already emitted `tapped` only for a permanent that changed from untapped to tapped (every tapping site checks it), which is the "untapped creature" of the cards.
- Decision: `GameEvent` `tapped` gets two optional fields, `by` (the player who tapped it) and `attacker` (true when it was tapped as it was declared as an attacker). They are set where a player taps: the `tap` effect and the tapping of chosen permanents (`res.controller`), the `{T}` cost of an ability, the tap cost of a crew or of another cost, the tapping for mana and the convoke-style tapping of a spell's cost (the player who pays), the declaration of attackers (the attacking player, with `attacker: true`, CR 508.1f) and regeneration (CR 701.19a: "its controller taps it"). A permanent that no player tapped carries neither. The trigger `becomesTapped` gets three optional fields: `by` (`you` or `opponent`, relative to the controller of the ability: the `by` of the event), `notAsAttacker` (leaves out a permanent with `attacker`: part of the event, read as it happens, not an intervening "if", CR 603.4) and `batch` ("one or more": the permanents tapped in the events of one step are one occurrence, `findTriggers` keeps the first one that the filter names, as for `diesBatch`, CR 603.2c). `by` and `batch` need a filter for the permanents (`validateCard`). Features: the trigger features `tappedBy`, `notAsAttacker` and `tapBatch`.
- Alternatives: a new trigger kind `youTap` next to `becomesTapped` (two kinds for one event, and the Verity Circle trigger would need the same fields); a cause enum on the event (`'effect' | 'cost' | 'mana' | 'attack'`: nothing reads the difference between an effect and a cost, and CR 701.26a says only that a player taps); `by` left out for attackers (the attacking player does tap them, CR 508.1f, and a "tap an untapped creature you control" trigger would then miss them).
- Consequences: "you tap an untapped creature" triggers for a tap by an effect, an ability cost or an attack declaration of the controller, never for a creature already tapped, and not for the tap of an opponent's own effect. The derive digests and the replay hashes move (the event has new fields, and the SYNTHETIC cards join the pools), so `ENGINE_VERSION` and `derive-digest.expected.json` are the integrator's.

## D-1501: "You may pay {1}. When you do, ..." is a `mayPay` whose effect is a `reflexive` ability with `afterPayment`, which may be modal (CR 603.12, 700.2b, 603.3c, 118.1)

- Context: Icewrought Sentry ("you may pay {1}{U}. When you do, tap target creature an opponent controls") and Hylda ("you may pay {1}. When you do, choose one — ...") create a reflexive triggered ability with the payment itself, with its own targets or its own modes. The engine had `mayPay` (whose effect runs after the payment, read as "If you do") and `reflexive` (created when an earlier instruction of the sequence did something, `res.performed`), but a payment is not an instruction that sets `performed`, and a reflexive ability could not be modal.
- Decision: the `reflexive` effect gets `afterPayment` (true: created whenever it runs, which is when the cost of the `mayPay` it is the effect of was paid; `validateCard` refuses it anywhere else and with `ifThisWay`) and `modes` with `chooseModes` (the reflexive ability is a modal triggered ability: its `effect` is the empty sequence, it declares no targets, the modes with their targets are chosen as it goes on the stack by the controller of the ability that created it, CR 603.3c; `validateCard` checks the modes as it does a triggered ability's, through the hook `Scope.checkModes`). Nothing else of the modal machinery changed: the pending trigger carries the ability with its modes, and `runQueue`/`answerTriggerModes` read them. The grammar: `lowerMayPay` reads "you may pay [cost]. When you do, [sentence]" (the sentence is lowered as every reflexive sentence is, with its own targets), and the segmenter reads "..., you may pay {1}. When you do, choose one —" with its bullets (`reflexiveModalHeader` and `reflexiveModes` of the skeleton, `IrAbility.reflexiveModal`), lowered by `lowerPaidModalTriggered`. Features: the ability features `reflexivePaid` and `reflexiveModal`.
- Alternatives: setting `performed` when a `mayPay` is paid (it would also make `didPrevious` and every later reflexive of the sequence read the payment: a payment is not "something the instruction did"); a new effect kind `payThenModes` (two kinds for the combination, and the modes machinery would be copied); modes on the `mayPay` itself (the modes belong to the reflexive ability that goes on the stack, not to the question).
- Consequences: any "you may pay {X}. When you do, [targeted effect or choose one]" of a triggered ability reads. A reflexive ability that is modal is the last effect of its `mayPay` and has no targets of its own besides its modes'. The casual agent answers the payment and the modes through the existing handlers.

## D-1502: "For each opponent, tap up to one target creature that player controls": the rule `effect.tap-per-opponent` (CR 115.1, 601.2c, 701.26a)

- Context: Hammers of Moradin. The slot `onePerOpponent` (D-149) is read by exile and destroy; the engine's `tap` effect takes any target slot.
- Decision: one grammar rule, `effect.tap-per-opponent`, reads the phrase for the verb tap with `perOpponentNoun` (exported from `rules/effect/angels.ts`); the lowering is the existing one (`declareTarget` writes `onePerOpponent`). The engine has two seats, so the slot takes at most one target.
- Alternatives: widening `effect.exile-per-opponent` to several verbs (its rejects and its feature names are about exile).
- Consequences: nothing in the engine changed for this card.

## D-1503: "Whenever you scry" and "up to X target nonland permanents, where X is the number of cards looked at while scrying this way" (CR 701.22, 603.3d)

- Context: Elvish Mariner. The engine had the event `scried` (`seat`, `top`, `bottom`) but no trigger for it, and no target slot whose size is a number of the event.
- Decision: the trigger `scries { who }` (`you`, `opponent`, `any`, relative to the controller; one occurrence for each `scried` event, so a scry of an empty library is one, CR 701.22d, and "scry 0" none, CR 701.22b), whose "that many" (`triggerDamage`, as for the life gained of `lifeGained`) is the number of cards looked at (`top + bottom`). The target slot gets `upToTriggerAmount` (true: none to that many targets; `findTriggers` writes the bounds into the ability it makes pending, `withTriggerAmount`; `validateCard` allows it only on a `scries` trigger, with no `min`, `max` or other count). An ability whose number is 0 is not put on the stack (up to 0 targets and nothing else to do: nothing happens either way, and the log is shorter). Grammar: `trigger.scry` and `effect.tap-up-to-x-scried` (`rules/effect/hylda-g1.ts`, one sentence with the `where` clause), the noun flag `upToTriggerAmount`, `LowerContext.scryTrigger`, and the feature `selector:upToTriggerAmount`.
- Alternatives: an `Amount` for the maximum of a slot (every slot would then read an amount as it is put on the stack, and the validator would have to say which amounts exist there); using `countX` (the X of a spell, announced before the targets: a trigger has no X, CR 107.3j).
- Consequences: unverified against the card's rulings (a cloud session cannot fetch Scryfall): the number of cards looked at is taken as the cards the library held, up to the number scried, which CR 701.22a and 701.22d give.

## D-1504: "Gains protection from the card type of your choice": the quality `cardTypeOfChoice`, the instruction `protectionCardType` and the decision purpose `protectionCardType` (CR 702.16a, 608.2d)

- Context: Pippin, Guard of the Citadel. D-451 did this for a color (`colorOfChoice`, the instruction `protectionColor`, the purpose `protectionColor`).
- Decision: the same, for the nine card types of `CARD_TYPES`: `{ kind: 'protection', from: 'cardTypeOfChoice' }` (an effect only, as the color's; a printed keyword and a static grant refuse it), the instruction `protectionCardType` (`rules/protection-choice.ts`), the `chooseOption` purpose `protectionCardType` with `options` the card types and `protects` the permanents the grant would affect, the `optionChosen` event of that purpose (public, like the color's), and `answerResolutionProtectionCardType`. The grant then runs as protection from that card type (`from: 'cardType'`), which every consumer of protection already reads (D-376). The grammar extends `effect.gain-protection` with "protection from the card type of your choice" (`IrKeywordArg.protection.choice` is `color` or `cardType`). The casual agent answers with the card type that threatens the protected permanents (`casual-protection.ts`, `chooseProtectionCardType`): 100 for each type of an opposing spell or ability on the stack that targets one of them, then 3 for a creature of the opponents and 1 for any other kind, lands excluded; ties to the first option, `creature` with nothing to count. The web asks it with the buttons of the named options (`option-names.ts`) and logs the choice.
- Alternatives: generalising the color instruction to a quality parameter (the decision purpose, the options and the log line differ, and each consumer would branch on the quality: the copy is smaller).
- Consequences: any "gains protection from the card type of your choice" reads. Not read: a group, another duration, "card types" in the plural.

## D-1505: A legendary creature named "<Name> of <Title>" calls itself by its first word (stage P32, D-147, D-198)

- Context: Sharae of Numbing Depths: "When Sharae enters, tap target creature ...". The self-reference rules knew the part of a legendary name before a comma, before " the ", and the given name of "<Given> <Family>" (D-198, which refuses a lower-case word after the first one so that "Gideon of the Trials" is not "Gideon", because a planeswalker's text also says "a Gideon planeswalker").
- Decision: `NormalizeOptions.creature` (set by `convertFace` from the face's types): for a legendary creature, "<Name> of <Title>" with a capitalised title and a first word of three letters or more that is no possessive and no permanent type is also called by the first word. The golden corpus changes for the seven cards of the group only, which shows the rule moves no other real card.
- Alternatives: dropping the lower-case guard of D-198 (breaks the planeswalkers); a list of names (a card-name special case).
- Consequences: a fixture of a real card that is a legendary "X of Y" creature compared by `override.ts` would normalise without the flag; none exists.
