# Hashaton deck, group 1: decisions (provisional numbers D-1100 to D-1103)

Issue 31, cards Faithful Mending, Archon of Cruelty, Archfiend of Ifnir and Psychic Frog. The numbers are provisional: the integrator renumbers them into `docs/DECISIONS.md` (code comments and docs use these numbers meanwhile). The Scryfall rulings could not be fetched in the session; the rules below were checked against the CR text of the owner's private rules repository (`pnpm rule 608.2c`, `pnpm rule 702.29`, `pnpm rule 701.9`).

## D-1100: A bare comma between the verbs of one player (2026-11-13)
- Context: "You gain 2 life, draw two cards, then discard two cards." (Faithful Mending) and "target opponent sacrifices a creature or planeswalker of their choice, discards a card, and loses 3 life" (Archon of Cruelty) join the verbs of one subject with a comma. `CLAUSE_JOIN` has ", and then", ", then", " and then", " then", ", and" and " and", but no bare ", ". The rest of both cards already read (the operand `a creature or planeswalker of their choice` is `noun.a-permanent`, "enters or attacks" is `trigger.or`).
- Decision: a new join `VERB_LIST_JOIN = choice(CLAUSE_JOIN, literal(', '))` in `rules/effect/operands.ts`, used only by the verb list of `effect.player` (the player subject). The longer joins are tried first, so ", and loses 3 life" and ", then discard" keep their connector. After a bare comma the next words must be a verb of the same subject (the verb agrees with "you" or with a third person, and is not a new subject), so "You draw a card, you discard a card." and "You draw a card, discards a card." stay unread. The instructions are one `sequence` in the order written (CR 608.2c).
- Alternatives: add ", " to `CLAUSE_JOIN` itself, which the sentence-level list of every effect also uses. Rejected: it would let a bare comma split any two effect clauses of the corpus (every effect rule's output would be tried after a comma), a far wider change than the cards need and one the golden diff could not review cheaply; a comma list of verbs of one player is the only shape in the group. A new `effect.player` rule for the comma list: rejected, the list already is the shape of `effect.player` and only its join differs.
- Consequences: two cards read with no engine change. Ugin, the Spirit Dragon's -10 ("You gain 7 life, draw seven cards, then put up to seven permanent cards from your hand onto the battlefield.") now reads up to "put up to seven permanent cards ..." and stops there, so the golden snapshot entry of Ugin changes its diagnostic (the card stays unsupported). A comma list whose later items are other clauses (a different subject, or no player) is still unread.

## D-1101: Combat damage "to a player or planeswalker" (2026-11-13)
- Context: Psychic Frog's "Whenever this creature deals combat damage to a player or planeswalker, draw a card." `trigger.combat-damage` read "to a player", "to an opponent", "to a creature" and no recipient; `dealsCombatDamage.to` was `'player' | 'opponent' | 'creature' | 'any'`, where `'any'` is also a creature or a battle.
- Decision: a fifth recipient `to: 'playerOrPlaneswalker'`. It matches a `damageDealt` event of combat damage whose recipient is a player, or a permanent that is a planeswalker, read as it is or as it last was when the damage already killed it (CR 113.7a, 120.3c: damage to a planeswalker removes loyalty, and a planeswalker with no loyalty is put into its owner's graveyard before the trigger is put on the stack). A creature and a battle are not recipients (CR 120.2a lists them apart). It has no "that player": a planeswalker may have been hit, so `triggerHasPlayer` is false for it and `validateCard` refuses `triggerPlayer` in the ability. The IR `damaged` gets the same value; the grammar reads " to a player or planeswalker" before " to a player" (the longer phrase first). Web: "Whenever ~ deals combat damage to a player or planeswalker".
- Alternatives: `'any'` for it, rejected (a blocked Frog would draw: damage to a creature is not a player or a planeswalker). Two triggers, one `player` and one for planeswalkers, rejected (damage to a player and a planeswalker in the same step would be two triggers where the card says one event for each recipient; and no trigger kind for "planeswalker only" exists). A filter on `any`, rejected as a bigger change to the type for one phrase.
- Consequences: Psychic Frog reads. "That player" after it is not read (flagged, never guessed); "a player or battle", "an opponent or planeswalker" and the like are not read (`docs/ROADMAP.md`).

## D-1102: "Whenever you cycle or discard another card" (2026-11-13)
- Context: Archfiend of Ifnir. `trigger.cycled` reads only "you cycle this card". CR 702.29a: cycling is an activated ability whose cost is to discard the card, so a cycled card is a discarded card; CR 702.29d: "Some cards have abilities that trigger whenever a player 'cycles or discards' a card. These abilities trigger only once when a card is cycled." The engine emits a `discarded` event for every discard (an effect, a cost, the cleanup step, cycling) and a `cycled` event after it for a cycled card.
- Decision: `Trigger` `discarded` gets `another?: boolean`, "a card other than the one with the ability" (`subject.id !== candidate.id`; no CR number defines "another", the repository reads it as every object but this one), and a new grammar rule `trigger.cycled-or-discarded` reads "you cycle or discard a card" and "you cycle or discard another card" as `discarded { who: 'you', another? }`. "Cycles or discards" needs no second event: one `discarded` event for a cycled card is one trigger (CR 702.29d), and the `cycled` event is left to "when you cycle this card". Web: "Whenever you discard another card" (cycling is discarding, so the words are true for the cycle).
- Alternatives: a new trigger kind `cycledOrDiscarded`, rejected (the same event as `discarded`, a second kind to validate, show and keep consistent). Counting both events (a `discarded` and a `cycled` occurrence), rejected: a cycled card would trigger twice, against CR 702.29d. Reading "another" with a position check on the battlefield, rejected: the object the card became in the graveyard never is the permanent of the ability, so the check is only observable for an ability that works from the graveyard, where the identity test is the right one.
- Consequences: Archfiend of Ifnir reads; any "whenever you cycle or discard a card" card reads (no `another`). The flag also goes with `who: 'opponent'` and `'any'` in the engine, which no rule reads. A card in the graveyard that sees "another card" is tested with SYNTHETIC `Grave Tither` and `Grave Other Tither`. Not read: "an opponent cycles or discards", a kind of card, "one or more cards", "you cycle a card" alone.

## D-1103: The `hashatonG1` AI soak style and the pool exclusion (2026-11-13)
- Context: playbook step 6 asks for a game test of the cards, and the generic AI pools must not change (`games-100.test.ts` expects `chooseOption`, `chooseX`, `chooseModes` and `choosePayOptions` only from dedicated styles; the SYNTHETIC cards of this group ask a `choosePermanent` and a `chooseCards` of the opponent in the middle of a resolution and put counters on every creature of a player whenever a card is discarded).
- Decision: `HASHATON_G1_NAMES` in `packages/ai/test/helpers.ts` lists the eight SYNTHETIC cards of the group and the FIXTURES chain filters them out of every generic pool, as the `NIV_GROUP_*_NAMES` lists do. A deck style `hashatonG1` (the cards twice, two cycling and planeswalker bodies, a little removal and card draw) and `packages/ai/test/hashaton-g1-games.test.ts` play it between random agents and `CasualAgent`: no fallback, no rejected action, invariants, exact replay, and two restated rules (the Frog triggers once for each combat hit on a player or planeswalker and never for a creature; the Fiend once for each card discarded for each Fiend its controller has).
- Alternatives: leave the cards in the generic pools, rejected (it changes seeded games and games-100's expectations). Test only by scenarios, rejected (no game-level check that the agents answer the opponent's sacrifice and discard questions).
- Consequences: no new decision kind, so no `CasualAgent` handler or overlay was needed; the soak runs 20 games on every run and 100 with `pnpm test:soak`.
