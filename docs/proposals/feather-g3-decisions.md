# Feather deck, group 3: decisions (provisional numbers D-1320 to D-1325)

Provisional numbers for `docs/DECISIONS.md`: the lead folds them in with the next free numbers. Issue 27, branch `support-feather`. Cards: Phalanx Leader, Tenth District Legionnaire, Danitha, Sword of Hope, Feather, the Redeemed, Zada, Hedron Grinder and Mirrorwing Dragon. None needed an S-class subsystem: the copies of a spell for each creature reuse the copy instruction (D-129), and the exile as a spell resolves reuses the Adventure/Dash pattern of the stack's `finish` (CR 715.3d, 702.109a). No proposal was written.

## D-1320: "A spell that targets this creature" and "that targets only this creature": `ObjectFilter.isSource` and `ObjectFilter.targetsOnly` (CR 115.1, 115.9b, 115.9c)
- Context: Phalanx Leader and Tenth District Legionnaire ("Whenever you cast a spell that targets this creature, ..."), Zada and Mirrorwing Dragon ("... that targets only this creature, ...") read what a cast spell targets, and the thing it must target is the permanent with the ability. `targetsObject` (D-206, D-400) takes an inner filter that cannot name "this creature", and says "at least one target matches", not "the targets are one object".
- Decision: two filter fields. `isSource: true` matches the object that is the source of the ability reading the filter (`filter-match.ts`, `ctx.source`); `validateCard` allows it only inside `targetsObject` or `targetsOnly`. `targetsOnly` is read like `targetsObject` (`select.ts`: stack objects only, same limits) but the chosen targets must be one object, chosen once or more (CR 115.9c: "if that number is one, even if the spell targets that object multiple times"), and that object matches the inner filter; a player target never does, a second different target does not. `targetsObject` and `targetsOnly` are not read together. The grammar reads "that targets ~" and "that targets only ~" in `trigger.cast` (IR `targetsSelf`, `targetsOnlySelf`; features `selector:isSource`, `selector:targetsOnly`). The target is read as it is now, or as it last was (`objectOrLki`), as `targetsObject` already does.
- Alternatives: a trigger flag "self" on `castSpell` (cannot say "only", and does not generalise to "a spell that targets another creature you control"); reading "targets only" as `targetsObject` plus a count (CR 115.9c counts different objects, not targets).
- Consequences: every cast trigger can read "this creature" as the target. The wording in the web says "this creature" when the card's name is known and "this permanent" otherwise.

## D-1321: A cast trigger's filter may be a disjunction with a member that says what the spell targets, and a copy of the spell for each other creature it could target: `copySpell.forEach` and `copier` (CR 707.10, 707.10e, 115.3)
- Context: Danitha, Sword of Hope ("an Equipment spell or a spell that targets a creature you control") needed `anyOf` with `targetsObject` in a member, which `matchesFilter` did not read (members were matched without the stack's targets). Zada and Mirrorwing Dragon copy the spell "for each other creature you control (they control) that the spell could target. Each copy targets a different one of those creatures.", the second copying under the control of the player who cast the spell ("that player copies").
- Decision, disjunction: `select.ts` matches the outer filter and then each member with the outer's zone and stack kind, so a member may say `targetsObject` or `targetsOnly`; `validateCard` lets a member of a disjunction on the stack read them; the grammar reads " or a spell that targets a creature [you control]" after the first spell phrase (a plain description; a choice such as "an Aura or Equipment spell" in front of it is not read). The IR and the lowering allow `targets` in a member only when the outer filter is on the stack.
- Decision, copies: the engine's `copySpell` gets `forEach: ObjectFilter` and `copier: 'triggerPlayer'`, and the instruction a stage `each` (`rules/copy-spell.ts`). The stage finds the permanents `forEach` names (read for the controller of the ability with the source as the source: `other` leaves out Zada; `controller: 'triggerPlayer'` is "they control" for the player who cast the spell), keeps those that are a legal target for every instance of the word "target" the spell's targets stand for (`legalTargets` of each slot of the spell, read for the copier with the spell as the source, so shroud, hexproof, protection and the wording of the slot count, CR 707.10e: otherwise the copy is not created), and queues one `make` for each with `retarget`: the copy has that one permanent as the target of every instance (CR 115.3 allows one object for several instances). Nobody chooses (`newTargets` is `no`, no `count`; `validateCard`). The copy belongs to `controller` (the instruction's field, the player the event is about for "that player copies") and the events (`spellCopied`, `becameTarget`) name that seat. The grammar reads the two sentences as one clause (`effect.copy-spell-each`, `segment.ts` joins "Each copy targets a different one of those creatures." to the sentence before it only when that sentence is the copy for each other creature).
- Order: the copies are put onto the stack in the order of the battlefield (oldest permanent first). CR 707.10 does not make the order a decision, and the copies are the controller's, all above the original; the order matters only when copies of one spell affect each other.
- Unverified: the Scryfall rulings of Zada and Mirrorwing Dragon (blocked in a cloud session). The behaviour follows CR 707.10e (one new target for every instance of "target") and 115.9c; whether the rulings say anything different was not checked.
- Alternatives: a new decision kind for the order or the choice of targets (no choice exists in the card text); asking the legality of each candidate after the copy exists (it would have to be removed again, and its events would have been emitted); a new instruction kind (the copy machinery is the same: `copyObject`, `makeCopy`, the stack's `copySpell` dispatch).
- Consequences: other "for each ... copy" effects (a copy for each opponent or each creature, "copy it for each other creature") can reuse `forEach` with another filter. A copy for each creature of an opponent is not read (the filter's `controller` is `you` or `triggerPlayer`).

## D-1322: "Exile that card instead of putting it into your graveyard as it resolves. If you do, return it to your hand at the beginning of the next end step": the effect `exileOnResolve` (CR 614.1a, 608.2n, 603.7, 603.7c)
- Context: Feather, the Redeemed. The trigger resolves while the spell it is about is still on the stack; the replacement applies later, when the spell resolves, and only then ("as it resolves"): a spell that is countered or fizzles goes to the graveyard and is not returned ("if you do" is false).
- Decision: `exileOnResolve { spell: triggerSubject }` writes `StackData.exileOnResolve { by, source }` on the spell (a spell card only: a copy ceases to exist and is no card). The stack's `finish` (`rules/stack.ts`, `rules/exile-on-resolve.ts`) sends an instant or sorcery that carries the mark to exile instead of its owner's graveyard, unless it would not go to a graveyard anyway (a permanent, an Adventure, flashback, "exile it instead" of D-352), and puts a delayed triggered ability on `delayedTriggers` for the card in exile: at the beginning of the next end step (every turn's, whoever's) `moveToZone` of the trigger subject to the hand, for `by`, with Feather as the source (CR 603.7c: the source may be gone). The card is read by its object in exile: one that left exile since is a new object and is not found (CR 400.7). The return to the hand of a commander asks the commander zone question as every move to the hand does (CR 903.9b). Two such returns waiting at the same end step are two distinct delayed triggers: their controller orders them (CR 603.3b).
- The grammar reads both sentences as one clause (`effect.exile-on-resolve`, `segment.ts` joins them): the second sentence alone, another return time or place, "that spell", "its owner's graveyard" are not read.
- Alternatives: `exileInstead` (D-352) on the spell, which also exiles a countered or fizzled spell (wrong per "as it resolves"); two effects (a replacement and a delayed return), which cannot express "if you do" across the resolution.
- Consequences: other "as it resolves, exile it instead / return it" effects (Rebound's exile, Jace's Mindseeker style) can reuse the mark with another destination and time. Rebound itself stays unbuilt (D-117).

## D-1323: The cast trigger of Danitha, Sword of Hope needed no `oncePerTurn` work (CR 603.2)
- Context: "This ability triggers only once each turn." The engine already has the flag (D-149, `Ability.oncePerTurn`, `limitOncePerTurn` in `rules/triggers.ts`) and the grammar reads the sentence (stage P33, D-150).
- Decision: the card's line is the disjunction of D-1321, "draw a card" and `oncePerTurn: true`; nothing else changed. A spell that is an Equipment and targets a creature you control is one event and one trigger.
- Consequences: none beyond D-1321.

## D-1324: Wording in the web, and an AI fix (CR 115.3)
- `filterPhrase` (`apps/web/src/game/trigger-text.ts`) says "a spell that targets only this creature" (the card's name when it is known, otherwise "this permanent") and a disjunction member by member ("an Equipment spell or a spell that targets a creature you control"); a shape the words do not cover gives `null`, as before (never guessed). `copySpell` with `forEach` is worded "Copy that spell for each other creature you control that the spell could target. Each copy targets a different one of those creatures" (and "That player copies ... they control ..."), only for that shape; `exileOnResolve` has its sentence (`ability-label.ts`, `copy-text.ts`). Before this, a `copySpell` with `forEach` would have been worded as a plain copy: that is why the wording was added with the engine change.
- `chooseCreatureToHelp` (`packages/ai/src/casual-helpful.ts`) filled only the first target slot of a pump spell; a pump spell with two slots (the SYNTHETIC `Paired Boost`, "target creature you control ... target creature with flying") was answered with one target and rejected by the engine. It now fills every slot with the agent's best own creature (the same creature may be the target of two slots, CR 115.3). One-slot spells behave as before.
