# Lorehold Spirit deck, group 1: provisional decisions D-1700 to D-1707

Group 1 of issue 86 (a Strixhaven precon): Claim Jumper, Containment Construct, Conspiracy Theorist, Perpetual Timepiece and Wave of Reckoning. The lead folds these entries into `docs/DECISIONS.md` with their final numbers. The real Oracle text is the one of `packages/cards/test/fixtures/corpus/golden.jsonl` (taken from the issue). The Scryfall rulings of the five cards were not fetched (blocked in a cloud session) and are unverified; the behaviour follows the Comprehensive Rules, read with `pnpm rule` from the owner's private rules repository (`COMMANDER_ARENA_CR_FILE`). None of the five cards needed a subsystem: no proposal.

## D-1700: "You may pay {1} and discard a card. If you do, ...": a discard in the payment of `mayPay` (CR 118.3, 701.9a, 608.2c)

- Context: Conspiracy Theorist ("Whenever this creature attacks, you may pay {1} and discard a card. If you do, draw a card."). `mayPay` (D-120) took mana and life only, and the grammar rule `effect.pay` read one mana cost or one payment of life. The payment code behind it (`rules/pay-unless.ts`, shared with Ward and `unlessPays`) already paid mana, life, energy and cards discarded: the cards first (all the hand when it holds exactly that many, else a `chooseCards` of purpose `discard`), the mana next.
- Decision: `mayPay` accepts a `discard` cost beside the mana (`ast/supported.ts`); the discard takes any cards of the hand, so a discard with a `filter` or a `named` is refused with a message. The payment is all or nothing (CR 118.3: a cost that cannot be paid is not paid): a player with no card in hand, or with no mana, is not asked. The grammar rule `effect.pay` reads "pay {1} and discard a card" (mana, " and ", a discard of a number of cards): the cost list `[mana, discard]`. Nothing else about the rule changed: "pay {1}, discard a card", a payment of life with a discard, "discard a creature card", "at random", "X cards" and a text with no "If you do" are not read.
- Alternatives: lowering "pay {1} and discard a card. If you do, ..." as `may { discard }` then `mayPay` (two questions, and the player could discard without paying); a new effect for the pair (the payment code was already there).
- Consequences: any "you may pay {mana} and discard N cards. If you do, ..." reads. The discard is a discard (CR 701.9a): it triggers "whenever you discard" (the Seer's second ability does so for its own payment, tested).

## D-1701: "Whenever you discard one or more nonland cards": the batch trigger `discarded` (CR 603.2c, 701.9a)

- Context: Conspiracy Theorist. The trigger `discarded` (D-206) triggers once for each card; "one or more" is a batch (CR 603.2c).
- Decision: the trigger `discarded` has `batch?: true`. The cards discarded in one run of events (a `discard` instruction, a cost, the cleanup step) that `who`, `another` and `filter` name are one occurrence: one pending trigger for each ability, however many cards. `findTriggers` writes the cards (as the objects they became in the graveyard, or in exile for madness) into the pending trigger's `cards`, as for `leftGraveyard` (D-232), so the effect can choose among them; `triggerSubject` is the first. `validateCard` gives the ability the scope `triggerCards`. Grammar: `trigger.discarded` also reads "one or more cards", "one or more nonland cards" and "one or more creature cards" (one kind, as before), for you, an opponent and a player, and lowers them with `batch`. The web says "Whenever you discard one or more nonland cards".
- Alternatives: a second trigger kind `discardedBatch` (the same fields); counting in the engine per step instead of per run of events (the engine's other batch triggers use the run of events).
- Consequences: two nonland cards discarded by one spell trigger the Seer once; the same two cards trigger a Golem twice (CR 603.2). The rule that "one run of events" is the instruction that discards (not the whole action) is a limit, see `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

## D-1702: "You may exile one of them from your graveyard": `exileFromTriggerCards` (CR 603.2c, 608.2d, 400.7)

- Context: Conspiracy Theorist's second ability: of the cards a batch trigger is about, the controller may exile one from their graveyard, and "if you do" may cast it.
- Decision: the effect `exileFromTriggerCards`, only in the ability of a batch `discarded` trigger (`validateCard`). It queues the engine's existing `chooseMove` instruction (D-139, D-411) to exile with `among` the cards of the trigger, `optional` (the choice of none or one card is the offer, so nothing is asked when no card of them is still in the graveyard), `count` 1. It resets `performed` as D-411's choice does, so `didPrevious` ("if you do") reads whether a card was exiled; the card is what `moved` names (`movesObjects` of the validator counts it, and also counts a `may` of a moving effect, so "you may exile that card. If you do, you may play that card" validates). Grammar: `effect.exile-one-of-them` ("exile one of them from your graveyard", after the sentence's "you may"), lowered by `lower/lorehold-g1.ts` only when the clause was optional and the ability's trigger is a batch discard (`LowerContext.discardBatchTrigger`); `lowerMay` and the trigger lowering do not wrap it in a `may` of its own.
- Alternatives: widening `exile` to a `choose` selector or `moveToZone` to `exile` (touches the many readers of both); a `may` that asks yes or no and then asks which card (two questions).
- Consequences: a card that left the graveyard in the meantime (another trigger exiled it first) is not offered (tested with the Golem and the Seer on one discard).

## D-1703: "You may play that card this turn" and "you may cast it this turn" after an exile of a card (CR 601.2, 305.1, 305.2, 611.2a)

- Context: Containment Construct ("Whenever you discard a card, you may exile that card from your graveyard. If you do, you may play that card this turn.") and Conspiracy Theorist ("... you may cast it this turn"). The engine's `mayPlay` (D-151) gives the permission for the card `moved` names, but the grammar read "play that card" only after an exile of the top card of the library or a mill, and not "cast" at all.
- Decision: (1) the grammar reads "that card" after an `exile` of the text: `LowerContext.exiled` gets the value `cards`, set when an `exile` effect is lowered, and `lower.may-play` accepts it (never "for as long as ... exiled", never "look at"). (2) `effect.may-play` reads "cast" as well as "play": "you may cast it this turn" is `mayPlay` with `castOnly: true`, a permission to cast that never plays a land. The `playPermission` of the continuous effect carries `castOnly`, and `canPlayLand` reads the permission with `asLand` (`mayPlayFromExile`, `mayPlayFromGraveyard`), so a land card exiled for a "cast" stays in exile, where a land exiled for a "play" can be played (and uses the land drop). A card exiled by a spell the same text names ("Exile target card from a graveyard. You may play that card this turn.") reads the same way. The web says "you may cast that card this turn".
- Alternatives: `mayPlay` with no flag and the card's types checked by the lowering (the trigger's filter says "nonland" but nothing ties the filter to the card exiled); reading "cast" as "play" (a land card could then be played, which the text does not allow).
- Consequences: "You may cast that card this turn" after the top card of the library is exiled is read now as well (a permission to cast): the golden corpus did not change for it. The text "Exile target creature. You may play that card this turn." reads too (a creature of another player exiled, the card is playable from exile by the controller).

## D-1704: "Shuffle any number of target cards from your graveyard into your library" (CR 701.24c, 701.24d, 115.1)

- Context: Perpetual Timepiece ("{2}, Exile this artifact: Shuffle any number of target cards from your graveyard into your library."). The engine had every piece: a target slot of any number (`TargetSpec.anyNumber`), `moveToZone` to the library, `shuffle`, the cost `exileThis` from the battlefield (D-229).
- Decision: grammar only. `noun.any-number-target-graveyard-cards` ("any number of target [creature] cards from your graveyard", your graveyard only) and `effect.shuffle-cards-into-library` ("shuffle <target cards of your graveyard> into your library": one target, any number, "up to N"), lowered to `moveToZone` of the target to the library and `shuffle` of the controller. The library is shuffled whatever the number of cards, also none (CR 701.24d: "Loaming Shaman ... no cards are targeted ... the targeted player will still have to shuffle"), because the shuffle is its own instruction. The web words the pair as one sentence, "any number of target cards" and "Exile this artifact".
- Alternatives: lowering to a single new effect (the two existing instructions say the same; the Hylda group 3 rule `effect.shuffle-into-libraries` lowers its shuffle of permanents the same way, with the owner's `shuffle`); a `moveToZone` with a position (the order is random anyway).
- Consequences: "Return any number of target cards from your graveyard to your hand" is not read (`effect.return` does not ask for the new noun); a card of another graveyard is not read.

## D-1705: "Each creature deals damage to itself equal to its power": the effect `dealDamageToSelf` (CR 120.3, 120.8, 608.2h)

- Context: Wave of Reckoning. `dealDamage` has one source and a recipient; `fight` and the bite have two creatures; no node had each object of a group as the source and the recipient of its own damage.
- Decision: `dealDamageToSelf { objects }` (an `each` selector or a reference, never a `choose`). As the effect begins, the objects on the battlefield with power above 0 are listed with their powers read from the state it began with (CR 608.2h: the information is determined once); then each is dealt damage by itself, one damage event for each, with the engine's own damage code (`dealDamageAsking`): deathtouch, lifelink, infect and wither of the creature apply (CR 120.3), a prevention or replacement effect for that source applies and asks as for any damage (the instruction returns the question and runs again), the damage is not combat damage, and a source with no power (0 or less) deals none (CR 120.8). Grammar: `effect.deal-damage-to-itself` ("each creature [you control | an opponent controls | other ...] deals damage to itself equal to its power") and the lowering to `each`. The web says "Each creature deals damage to itself equal to its power".
- Alternatives: extending `dealDamage.from` with an `each` selector (a recipient "itself" for each source: a new recipient kind and a loop in a node that is read in many places); lowering to a `sequence` of one `dealDamage` for each creature (the group is not known as the card is lowered).
- Consequences: every creature of the battlefield, whoever controls it, takes its own power (the small ones die, the big ones survive); a creature with deathtouch and power 1 or more destroys itself (CR 704.5h), tested.

## D-1706: "If you search your library this way, shuffle." and "repeat this process once": `thisWay` `searched` and the repeat of an offered search (CR 701.23a, 701.24a, 608.2c, 603.4)

- Context: Claim Jumper ("When this creature enters, if an opponent controls more lands than you, you may search your library for a Plains card and put it onto the battlefield tapped. Then if an opponent controls more lands than you, repeat this process once. If you search your library this way, shuffle."). Three things were missing: the put joined with "and" in the search rule, the repeat, and a shuffle that depends on having searched.
- Decision: (1) `effect.search` reads a put joined with "and" for one card ("search your library for a Plains card and put it onto the battlefield tapped"), with no reveal and no shuffle in the sentence (the older forms with commas and "then shuffle" are read as before). (2) `thisWayAtLeast` of the new `ThisWayKind` `searched`: the `search` instruction adds 1 when the player went through with a search (a "may" search that was accepted, with or without a card found, CR 701.23a, 701.23c: with nothing to find the player still searched); the grammar's `condition.searched-this-way` ("you search your library this way") lowers to it, only after a search of the same text (`LowerContext.didSearch`). (3) A "you may" search of a text that repeats is the engine's `search` with `optional` (a question for each search, the question a declined search answers is "no search": nothing is found and nothing is shuffled), not the "may" of the whole ability: `lowerOfferedSearch` lowers it, and the trigger lowering treats it as asking itself (`asksItself`). (4) `effect.repeat-process` ("repeat this process once") is lowered by `lowerRepeatProcess`: the effect of the one sentence before it, done again, in a `conditional` on the test the sentence says ("Then if an opponent controls more lands than you", read as the instruction comes up, CR 608.2c), or alone with no test. A text with more than one sentence to repeat, a process that chooses targets, or a number other than "once" is refused: the words do not say how far "this process" goes, and a second choice of targets is a second question.
- Alternatives: a new effect `repeat` with a count and the effects inside (the lowering would still have to find the extent of the process); putting the shuffle inside each search (the shuffle is one, after both, and it is not done when neither search was made).
- Consequences: the shuffle is made once when one search or two were made, never when both were declined (tested at the scenario and in the soak, with the rules read again in the test); a Land Claimer of a game with the opponent still ahead after the first Plains asks again, once.

## D-1707: Mutation checks, the golden corpus and the unverified rulings

- Context: the checks the work order asks for, and what the lead regenerates.
- Decision: eleven mutations, each on a scratch copy of the file (restored after) and each killed by the tests of the group: `dealDamageToSelf` reads toughness for the amount, or deals combat damage; the batch trigger is not deduplicated (one trigger for each card); `exileFromTriggerCards` is not optional; the cast-only permission lets a land be played; a search, or an empty search, does not count as `searched`; the cost list of `effect.pay` loses the discard; "cast it" is read as "play it"; `lowerRepeatProcess` repeats more than one sentence; the shuffle of the Timepiece's lowering is dropped. The golden corpus changes for exactly five cards, all `unsupported -> supported`: Claim Jumper, Conspiracy Theorist, Containment Construct, Perpetual Timepiece and Wave of Reckoning (no other card of the corpus changed status or AST). The existing test "out of scope stays unparsed" used "Search your library for a basic land card and put it onto the battlefield tapped." as an example of text the grammar does not read; the grammar reads it now (D-1706), so the example is "Search your library for two basic land cards and put them onto the battlefield tapped." (still not read: a put joined with "and" is for one card). `ENGINE_VERSION` and `PARSER_VERSION` are not bumped here (the lead does), and the derive digests are not regenerated.
- Alternatives: none.
- Consequences: the lead regenerates `golden.expected.json` (`UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 pnpm exec vitest run packages/cards/test/golden.test.ts`) and bumps the versions; the derive digests move only if `ENGINE_VERSION` does; the lead checks them after the bump (this group did not run them).
