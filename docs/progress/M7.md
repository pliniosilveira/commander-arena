# M7 progress report: the stronger AI

Date: 2026-10-30. M7 ran as four slices on four branches: M7a `m32-ai-determinize` (merged), M7b `m33-ai-arena` (merged), M7c `m34-heuristic-agent` (pull request 66) and M7d `m35-web-ai` (this branch, built on M7c). This report closes the milestone once those are merged.

## Done-when

SPEC 15 and A11 ask for `HeuristicAgent` to beat `GreedyAgent` in 70% of 200 games, `RandomLegalAgent` in 95%, and mirrored self-play to land between 40% and 60% per seat. **Measured on the arena decks, it is met** (numbers below, from D-163), with two cautions that this report states plainly: the arena decks are 24-card lists that end by decking, and the mirror check cannot fail (see "Known gaps"). On the real 100-card precons the agent beats the casual agent in 55% to 61% of games and plays every game to a result.

## What each slice delivered

- **M7a (D-161), `determinize`.** `determinize(state, viewer, rng)` in the engine: a copy of the real state in which what the viewer cannot know (the other seats' hands, libraries and face-down exile, the order of its own library, the seed) is drawn again. `DecideContext.sample(seed)` hands it to an agent, built lazily by `runGame` and by the web host. SPEC amendment A31: the AI may know the opponent's decklist, never a hand or a library order.
- **M7b (D-162), the means to measure.** `evaluate` (a board score that is one side's advantage, with labelled terms), reason codes and `ExplainingAgent`, `GreedyAgent` (the A11 benchmark), `playMatch` (paired seeds, seats swapped) and `pnpm ai:arena`.
- **M7c (D-163), `HeuristicAgent`.** Blocks and attacks are played out on the sample with the engine and scored by the evaluator, with a crack-back penalty, a learned opponent block model and a library clock; mana is planned as the set of spells that uses the most; removal is aimed with "do not waste it" filters; instants wait for three windows; mulligans by land count and curve. Levels `normal` and `easy`. Every decision kind it does not take over is answered by the casual handlers.
- **M7d (D-164), the agent in the app.** The Worker host builds the opponent from `NewGameOptions.ai`; the menu and the "Play this deck" panel carried an Easy and Normal setting (since removed, D-168); `?autostart` plays the casual agent unless `&ai=` says otherwise, so the existing tests keep their opponent; the time bound on big boards; `playMatch` with a deck per seat, `pnpm ai:arena --precons`, and the precon soak test.

## Known gaps

- On boards of 70 or more permanents the engine's `derive` makes every engine step slow, and the agent drops candidates by the clock and answers the rules-based plan. A cheaper `derive` is the real fix: done afterwards in D-173 (a turn on a 58-permanent board 4.5 times faster, the same games and the same win counts).
- On the precons the edge over the casual agent is smaller than on the arena decks (55% to 61%, against about 61%), and Easy was about even with the casual agent. The agent's constants are tuned on the arena decks.
- A mirror between two agents of one kind plays each seed twice identically, so wins per agent are 50% by construction (this also holds for the mirror numbers of D-163): only the split by seat means something.
- SPEC 9's delay so a human can follow the AI's plays is not built; replay import is not built (a replay of a game whose AI hit its time limit may differ).
- The agent's own limits (no threat assessment across several opponents, three instant windows, activated abilities left to the casual rules) are in `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

## After the milestone: issue 73

A game against the web opponent showed the agent attacking with its commander into a blocker that kills it for free. The block planner gave a lone blocker to the strongest attacker it merely stops instead of the weaker one it kills, and the same planner models the opponent when attacks are played out. D-170 has the diagnosis and the fix (two passes in tier 1 of the block plan); the arena numbers above did not change (`node tools/ai/arena.ts` with the seeds and opponents of D-163 and D-164 were rerun and are in D-170).
