# The deck-support routine plans, then builds independent groups in parallel

- Context: issue 281 measured the routine at one card per 20 to 40 minutes in a single session (from the commit timestamps of the Kambal and Lord of the Nazgûl pull requests), so a deck of about 20 unsupported cards takes 8 to 12 hours. Most gap groups do not depend on each other. The Hylda build had fanned out once with worktree subagents, but the skill did not say how, so it was not the default.
- Decision: `.claude/skills/deck-support/SKILL.md` has Step 2b. (1) The lead plans in one pass: diagnostics for every unsupported card, groups by gap class with their dependencies, the checklist in the pull request description, and a budget read before the fan-out. (2) It fans out when there are at least 3 independent T, P or K groups: at most 4 subagents at a time, each in its own worktree and branch cut from the deck branch, following the playbook and the local check loop (`pnpm test:focus`, the package typecheck). (3) Version constants, the golden snapshot and the derive digests belong to the lead and, as for a serial build, are bumped and regenerated once at the end of the pull request; the lead merges one group at a time and reads the diff; a subagent reports the text of any decision it would record and the lead writes the file. (4) S-class groups never fan out. (5) The first decks built this way report their wall-clock time next to a comparable serial build.
- Alternatives: fan out every deck (a deck of one or two groups pays the merge and review cost for nothing); no cap on agents (tokens per hour and merge load grow with it); let subagents bump versions (every merge would conflict).
- Consequences: a large deck can finish in a fraction of the wall-clock time, at a higher token use per hour and about the same in total (tracked in issue 287). The gain is an estimate until a real deck is built this way and its pull request reports the two times. Issue 281's acceptance asks for that deck; it is still to be done.
